Scene

Hajime and Yasumi, old friends, catching up.

Hajime

そういえば、先月せんげつ川越かわごえに引ひっ越こしたんだよ。

Oh, by the way, I moved to Kawagoe last month.

Yasumi

えっ、川越かわごえ？ずいぶん遠とおくない？

What, Kawagoe? Isn’t that a long way out?

Hajime

まあね。でも、都心としんから少すこし離はなれると、家賃やちんは安やすいし、部屋へやも広ひろいんだ。

Sort of. But go a little way out of central Tokyo, and the rent’s cheaper and the rooms are bigger.

Hajime

それで、今度こんど引ひっ越こしパーティーをやるんだけど、来こない？

So I’m having a housewarming party soon. Want to come?

Yasumi

行いく行いく！でも川越かわごえって行いったことないんだよね。何線なにせんで行いけばいいの？

I’m in! But I’ve never been to Kawagoe. Which line do I take?

Hajime

東武線とうぶせんで行いくと、すぐだよ。新しん宿じゅくから山手線やまのてせんに乗のって、池いけ袋ぶくろで降おりて。

Go by the Tōbu line, and it’s no time at all. Take the Yamanote Line from Shinjuku, and get off at Ikebukuro.

Hajime

そこで東武東上線とうぶとうじょうせんの、小川町おがわまち行いきに乗のり換かえる。そうすると、乗のり換かえなしで川越かわごえまで行いけるよ。

There, change to the Tōbu Tōjō Line, a train bound for Ogawamachi. Then it takes you all the way to Kawagoe, no more changes.

Hajime

駅えきからうちまでは道みちがややこしいから、地図ちずをメールで送おくるね。

The way from the station to my place is confusing, so I’ll email you a map.

Yasumi

了りょう解かい！頼たのんだよ。楽たのしみにしてるね！

Got it! I’m counting on you. Can’t wait!

Hajime

あ、駅えきに着ついたら電話でんわしてね。迷まよったら迎むかえに行いくから。

Oh, and call me when you get to the station. If you get lost, I’ll come and find you.

2

The scene

Naomi, Yuichi and Peter, before and after the dialogue

Part one of “Party in the ’Burbs”. Whose story is it? Hajime and Yasumi’s, Naomi says. And who are they? No idea, Naomi admits, but they must be friends, Peter reasons, because they talk casually. They’re talking about trains, and are they on the phone? No idea either. The grammar point is と, for “when” or “if”: the と conditional.

After the dialogue

Has Yuichi been to Kawagoe? Yes, to see the old streets of Koedo (小江戸の街並み): Kawagoe is sometimes called 小江戸, “little Edo”, Naomi explains. It’s famous for sweet potatoes too, and they’re delicious. When Peter asks again whether Yuichi has been, Naomi snaps back だからあるって言ったじゃん, and Yuichi and Naomi tell Peter to listen properly. Peter did hear, but doesn’t quite believe it: why would a 21-year-old go out there? Yuichi has friends there, and has been twice. Why not somewhere younger, like Shibuya or Shinjuku, Peter wonders; of course Yuichi goes there too. Lots of tourists come to Kawagoe to see Koedo, Yuichi says, and the Kawagoe festival is famous, Naomi adds: for its portable shrines, perhaps. For its floats (だし), Yuichi says, and lots of street stalls (屋台), in the daytime too, on festival days. Peter doesn’t look very interested, Naomi notices; after Yuichi’s talk, Peter is still not that keen, but a little more interested than before. Naomi promises a link to Kawagoe city’s website. (Section 8 has more on Kawagoe.)

Phrases worth taking from the chat:

  • だからあるって言ったじゃん — “Yuichi just said so!”: だから, “like I said”; あるって言った, “said ‘I have’”; and the casual じゃん, “didn’t…?”
  • ちゃんと聞いてて下さい — “please listen properly”: ちゃんと, properly; 聞いてて, short for 聞いていて.
  • 聞いてますけども、信じていないです — “I’m listening, but I don’t believe it”.
  • もちろん、そっちも行ってます — “of course I go there too”: そっち, casual for そちら.
  • 興味なさそうですね — “you don’t look very interested”: ない + そう becomes なさそう.
  • 興味を持つようになりました — “I’ve come to take an interest”: 〜ようになる, from lesson 9.
  • さよなら！ — Yuichi’s sign-off this time.

Key lines

Hajime’s reason, and the directions

The lesson’s dialogue is nine lines long, so these three are quoted exactly; section 1 covers the rest in my retelling. The English is my own rendering.

うん、川越かわごえまで行いくと家賃やちん安やすいし広ひろいところを借かりられるからね。

Yeah. Go as far as Kawagoe and the rent’s cheap, and you can rent somewhere big. — Hajime

Grammar: 行くと, the と conditional (section 5); 家賃安い, with が dropped, as in speech; し, “and also, what’s more” (lesson 7); 借りられる, the potential; からね, giving the reason.

東武線とうぶせんを使つかうと早はやいよ。新しん宿じゅくで池いけ袋ぶくろ行いきの山手線やまのてせんに乗のる。

The Tōbu line’s quickest. At Shinjuku, take the Yamanote Line bound for Ikebukuro. — Hajime

Grammar: 使うと早い, “if you use it, it’s quick”; 池袋行き, “bound for Ikebukuro”; 山手線に乗る, with に marking the train; the plain 乗る, giving directions step by step.

池いけ袋ぶくろで東武東上線とうぶとうじょうせんに乗のり換かえて、小川町おがわまち行いきに乗のる。そうすると、川越かわごえに着つくよ。

At Ikebukuro, change to the Tōbu Tōjō Line and take a train bound for Ogawamachi. Do that, and you’ll get to Kawagoe. — Hajime

Grammar: 乗り換えて, the て-form linking steps; そうすると, “do that, and…”, the と conditional opening a sentence; 川越に着く, to arrive in Kawagoe.

Vocabulary

The lesson’s word list, in its order

The hosts’ notes first, then a card for each word, plus more from the dialogue and the chat, each with an example of my own.

  • ひっこす (引っ越す) — to move, to change your address. The ます-form is 引っ越します, and 引っ越し works as a noun: 引っ越しはいつですか？ means the same as いつ引っ越しますか？, “when are you moving?” In Japan, April is moving month, Peter says, since school starts in April, Naomi adds, and so does everything new. And new employees too, Peter goes on; in fact, since people start at new companies and schools in April, it’s really March that’s the moving month.
  • やちん (家賃) — rent. 家 is house or home, and 賃 a fare or fee, Yuichi explains: the money for the house. 家賃が高い, the rent is expensive; 家賃が安い, the rent is low. Careful, Naomi says: you can’t say 家賃が低い; it has to be 安い, “cheap”.
  • せん (線) — a line: a phone line or a railway line. 山手線 is the Yamanote Line, and the Tōbu Tōjō Line, Yuichi supplies, is 東武東上線. The Yamanote is the most famous line, Peter declares. Among whom, Naomi asks? Among Japanese people, say Yuichi and Peter, and Peter decides Naomi-sensei still has a lot to learn.
  • のる (乗る) — to get on, to take; に marks the means of transport: 電車に乗る, to get on a train; タクシーに乗る, to take a taxi; ヘリコプターに乗る, to get in a helicopter.
  • のりかえる (乗り換える) — to transfer, to change trains. It’s two words, Yuichi explains: のり, the stem of 乗ります, and かえる, to change. You use it as 〜から…に乗り換える: バスから電車に乗り換える, to change from the bus to the train; 東京駅で新幹線から電車に乗り換えて下さい, “at Tokyo Station, change from the Shinkansen to a local train.”
  • ややこしい — confusing, puzzling; like 複雑, complicated, or わかりにくい, hard to follow: ややこしい文法, confusing grammar; ややこしい問題, a complicated problem. Yuichi’s example, 男女関係はややこしい, “relationships between men and women are complicated”, works anywhere in the world (世界で通用します). Can you call a person ややこしい, Peter asks? Better to be specific, Naomi says: an explanation, or an opinion, can be ややこしい.
  • たのむ (頼む) — to ask, to request: 寿司をたのんだ, “I ordered sushi.”

That’s the lesson notes’ list too; the cards below cover it, with more words from the dialogue and the chat.

Beyond the lessonややこしい is defined as complicated, tangled and tiresome: 話がややこしくなってきた, “things are getting complicated”. One dictionary’s earliest example, from a comic story of the 1860s, could be Yuichi’s: どうで男といふものは、どこでも皆ややこしい事してくるでゑな, roughly “men will be men: wherever they go, they all get up to complicated business.” The dictionaries give it no origin; the popular story that it comes from ややこ, an old word for a baby, isn’t in them.

家賃 and other fees. The 賃 of 家賃 is money paid for work or for the use of something, so it also turns up in 賃金, wages; 運賃, the fare for the train to Kawagoe; and 賃貸, renting out.

頼む, and Yasumi’s 頼んだ. Ordering is in the dictionary, as sending for someone or something: タクシーを頼む, to call a taxi; 出前を頼む, to order a delivery. Yasumi’s 頼んだ！ is another sense, leaving something in someone’s hands, as in あとを頼む, “I’ll leave the rest to you.” And 乗り換える stretches beyond trains: 条件のいい仕事に乗り換える, to switch to a job with better terms.

5

〜と, if and when

The grammar point: the と conditional

と, Peter explains, is a conjunction for making conditional sentences: a clause, then と, then the main clause. If or when the first part is fulfilled, the second part takes place, and that’s the key: the first part has to happen for the second to follow. Yuichi’s example: たくさん食べると太る, “if you eat a lot, you put on weight”; in Japanese order, “a lot, eat, if: put on weight”. Naomi’s: 4月になると、学校が始まる, “when April comes, school starts”: the second part only happens once the first is fulfilled.

1

Plain non-past + と + what follows

春になると暖かくなる, “when spring comes, it gets warmer”. What follows does so naturally, every time.

2

Non-past even for the past

窓を開けると、雪が降っていた, “when I opened the window, it was snowing”. The verb before と stays non-past; the main clause carries the tense.

3

No requests after it

The main clause can’t ask, suggest or invite. For that, use たら.

The lesson notes fill in the rest. It’s sometimes called the “to-conditional”, and depending on the context it isn’t really a condition at all, just “when this happens, that happens”. Their examples of the main-clause rule: 夜になると、電話してください is wrong, while 夜になると、虫が鳴き始めた, “when night fell, the insects started to sing”, is fine. The request needs たら: 夜になったら、電話してください.

When spring comes, the cherry trees along the river all bloom at once.

Watch forと can’t end in a request, an invitation or your own plan: not 駅に着くと電話してください, but 駅に着いたら電話してください, as Hajime says at the end of section 1.

と, ば, たら, なら

Beyond the lesson: four ways to say “if”, and when to use which

Beyond the lessonJapanese has four everyday ways to say “if”, and learners mix them up for years. They overlap a lot, so the useful question is what each one can’t do.

FeelRequest after it?Example
とalways, naturally, by ruleno押おすと開あく
ばa condition; sayingsnot after an action安やすければ買かう
たらthe all-rounder, in speechyes着ついたら電話でんわして
なら“if that’s so”, adviceyes行いくなら東武線とうぶせんだよ

Beyond the lessonと: what always follows. A Japan Foundation grammar column for teachers says the second half of a と sentence is something thought to happen naturally, or without fail, as a result of the first, which is why it’s taught for directions, for how machines work, and for the weather and the seasons. Habits are fine (毎朝、会社へ行くと、Ｅメールを送ります, “every morning, when I get to work, I send emails”), but not plans: put 明日 in place of 毎朝, and the sentence stops working. Requests, invitations and intentions all fail after と, and every one of the column’s failed examples, such as 明日晴れると、テニスをしましょう, becomes correct with たら.

たら: the all-rounder. The column’s piece on たら calls it the one said to have no limits on use, handy in all kinds of situations, with one catch: with a past main clause, the second half can’t be something the speaker did on purpose. 会社を出たら、ちょうどタクシーが来た, “just as I left the office, a taxi came”, works; 会社を出たら、タクシーに乗った doesn’t. And a study of novels found that past と belongs mostly to narration, as in Kawabata’s 目がさめると，庭で犬が吠えていた, “when I woke, a dog was barking in the garden”, while in conversation たら does most of the work.

ば: it depends on the verb. After an action verb, ば can’t lead into a command: 辞書を使えば、日本語の新聞が読めるだろう, “with a dictionary, you should be able to read a Japanese paper”, is fine, but not 辞書を使えば、もっと日本語を勉強しなさい. After a state, anything goes: このスカートが安ければ、買おうと思います, “if this skirt were cheap, I’d buy it.”

なら: picking up what was said. Grammarians describe なら as taking up what the other person has just said: 「海に行きたいなあ」「海なら、沖縄がいいですよ」, “I’d love to go to the seaside.” “If it’s the sea you want, Okinawa’s good.” That’s why it suits advice, and why it can’t describe a result that simply follows: フランスに行けば、凱旋門を見ることができます, “go to France and you can see the Arc de Triomphe”, works only with ば.

安やすければ買かうけど、この値段ねだんじゃちょっと……。

If it were cheap I’d buy it, but at this price…

「川越かわごえに行いきたいんだけど。」「川越かわごえなら、東武東上線とうぶとうじょうせんが便利べんりだよ。」

“I want to go to Kawagoe.” “For Kawagoe, the Tōbu Tōjō Line is handiest.”

Trains and moving house

Beyond the lesson: Hajime’s route, the words on the platform, and moving season

Beyond the lessonHajime’s route, checked. The Yamanote Line is a loop of 30 stations, about 34.5 km round, and a full circuit takes about an hour: 64 minutes on JR East’s weekday timetable. Trains in Japan keep to the left, so the clockwise trains, on the outside of the loop, are the 外回り, and the anticlockwise ones the 内回り. From Shinjuku to Ikebukuro, take the outer loop: about eight minutes. Ikebukuro is where the Tōbu Tōjō Line begins, running 75 km out into western Saitama to Yorii, and Kawagoe is about 30 minutes away by express, the city says. Kawagoe also has JR’s Kawagoe Line from Ōmiya, and the Seibu Shinjuku Line, whose trains end at 本川越.

The busiest station on earth. Guinness World Records lists Shinjuku as the world’s busiest station, with an average of 2,704,703 passengers a day in 2022, still about 20% below its pre-pandemic level. The runner-up, Paris’s Gare du Nord, handles around 600,000.

Express or rapid? Dictionaries define 急行 as stopping only at major stations, 快速 as making fewer stops to go faster, and 準急 as stopping a little more often than an express, but none ranks 急行 against 快速: each company decides. Tōbu’s own guide to the Tōjō Line lists TJライナー, 川越特急, 快速急行, 急行, 準急 and 普通, and the line dropped its plain 快速 in 2023. On private lines, a commuter 特急 or 急行 usually needs no extra ticket; one with special carriages for day-trippers does. Even 普通列車 is defined two ways: by fare, a train you can ride on a basic ticket, or by stops, one that halts at nearly every station.

Tap and go. PASMO started on 18 March 2007, the same day Suica and PASMO began working on each other’s lines, and since March 2013 the main transport IC cards have worked across the country.

Peter’s moving month. Peter’s correction holds: March wins. In 2025, 905,179 people moved between municipalities in March, against about 433,000 in an average month and 309,853 in November, the quietest. April came second, with 683,859. Moving firms struggle to keep up: since 2019 the transport ministry has asked people to spread their moves out, because March brings about twice the jobs of a normal month, and finding enough drivers and lorries for the peak has become hard.

Renting a flat. Cheap rent isn’t the only cost of moving in. A ministry guidebook for foreign renters explains 敷金, a deposit held against unpaid rent or repairs, usually one or two months’ rent, settled and refunded when you leave; and 礼金, “key money”, paid to the landlord when you sign, common in the Kantō region, usually one or two months’ rent and never returned, though some flats now ask for none. The rent itself, 家賃, is usually paid by the end of each month for the month ahead.

Words on the platform

  • 〜行き — bound for; 〜番線, platform number: 池袋行きは三番線です.
  • 各駅停車 (普通) — stops at every station; 急行, 快速 and 準急 skip some, and which ones depends on the company.
  • 乗り換え — a change of trains; 乗り過ごす, to miss your stop.
  • 始発 and 終電 — the first train and the last one.
  • 改札 — the ticket gates, where you tap your IC card.

池いけ袋ぶくろから急きゅう行こうに乗のると、川越かわごえまで三さん十じゅっ分ぷんぐらいだよ。

Take an express from Ikebukuro, and it’s about half an hour to Kawagoe.

話はなしているうちに、降おりる駅えきを乗のり過すごしてしまった。

I was so busy talking that I missed my stop.

8

Kawagoe, Little Edo

Beyond the lesson: Yuichi’s day out, the sweet potatoes and the festival

Beyond the lessonWhy “Little Edo”? Kawagoe was a castle town guarding the northern approach to Edo, held by the shogun’s trusted lords, and the Kawagoe road and boats on the Shingashi River tied it to the capital. Silk, cotton, paulownia chests, sweets, rice, tea and sweet potatoes passed through its merchants’ hands, and it prospered enough to be called 小江戸. The name isn’t Kawagoe’s alone: the dictionary defines a 小江戸 as any town that keeps the look of Edo times, with Hikone as another example.

Built against fire. On 17 March 1893, a great fire burned 1,302 houses, a third of the town. The few fire-resistant 蔵造り buildings proved their worth, so the merchants rebuilt in that style, finished in black plaster. Since 1 December 1999, about 7.8 hectares of those streets have been a protected district of historic buildings. Its landmark, the bell tower 時の鐘, is said to have been first built between 1627 and 1634; it burned in 1893 and was rebuilt the next year. Today it rings four times a day, at 6 a.m., noon, 3 p.m. and 6 p.m., and it’s on a government list of 100 soundscapes of Japan. Nearby, the old sweets alley 菓子屋横丁, some thirty shops on a stone-paved lane, made a list of 100 fragrant places.

Better than chestnuts. Roast-sweet-potato sellers in Edo hung up signs reading 十三里, “thirteen ri”, a pun the dictionaries spell out: 栗（九里）より（四里）うまい, “tastier than chestnuts”, where 栗 sounds like 九里, “nine ri”, and より like 四里, “four ri”, and nine plus four makes thirteen. Saitama Prefecture adds that thirteen ri was the distance from Edo to Kawagoe, about 51 km, and Kawagoe city says its potatoes, shipped to Edo by river, were counted the best.

The festival. The 川越まつり is held every October, on the third Sunday and the day before; this year, that’s 17 and 18 October 2026. The hosts are both partly right. It began in 1648, when the lord of Kawagoe gave the Hikawa Shrine a portable shrine, lion’s head and drums, and that procession is still part of it; but it’s famous for its floats. Its official name, 川越氷川祭の山車行事, means “the float event of the Kawagoe Hikawa festival”; it has been a national important intangible folk cultural property since 2005, and since 2016 on UNESCO’s list as one of Japan’s Yama, Hoko, Yatai float festivals. The highlight is the 曳っかわせ, when floats meet face to face and their bands play against each other, loudest at night. In 2025 the festival drew 661,000 visitors, and Kawagoe as a whole 7.16 million.

川越かわごえに行いくと、蔵造くらづくりの町並まちなみが見みられる。

Go to Kawagoe, and you can see streets of old warehouse-style shops.

秋あきになると、焼やき芋いもが食たべたくなる。

When autumn comes, I start wanting roast sweet potatoes.

Quiz

Pick the word or form that fits. Each answer comes with the reason.

1. 春はるになる＿＿＿、桜さくらが咲さく。

When spring comes, the cherry blossoms bloom.

Something that always follows: と.

2. このボタンを押おす＿＿＿、お湯ゆが出でます。

Press this button and hot water comes out.

How a machine works, every time: と.

3. 駅えきに＿＿＿と、電車でんしゃはもう出でていた。

When I got to the station, the train had already left.

The verb before と stays in the plain non-past, even for a past event.

4. 家いえに帰かえる＿＿＿、母ははが料理りょうりを作つくっていた。

When I got home, my mother was cooking.

と with a past main clause: what you found when it happened.

5. 駅えきに＿＿＿、電話でんわしてください。

Please call me when you get to the station.

A request can’t follow と, or ば after an action verb: use たら.

6. 池いけ袋ぶくろで乗のり換かえて、三みっつ目めの駅えきで降おりる。＿＿＿、目めの前まえが会かい場じょうだよ。

Change at Ikebukuro and get off at the third stop. Then the venue’s right in front of you.

そうすると: “do that, and then…”, the と conditional at the start of a sentence.

7. この辺あたりは、家賃やちんが＿＿＿。

Rents are cheap around here.

Rent is 安い or 高い, never 低い, Naomi says.

8. 毎朝まいあさ、バス＿＿＿乗のって会社かいしゃに行いく。

Every morning I take the bus to work.

乗る marks the vehicle with に.

9. 新しん宿じゅくで、JR＿＿＿地下鉄ちかてつに乗のり換かえた。

At Shinjuku I changed from the JR line to the subway.

AからBに乗り換える: to change from A to B.

10. 東とう京きょう＿＿＿の電車でんしゃは、二番線にばんせんから出でます。

The train for Tokyo leaves from platform 2.

〜行き: bound for.

11. この説せつ明めいは＿＿＿、よく分わからない。

This explanation is confusing; I don’t really get it.

ややこしい: complicated, hard to follow.

12. 晩ばんご飯はんに、ピザを＿＿＿。

I ordered pizza for dinner.

頼む: to ask for, and so to order.

13. 来月らいげつ、駅えきの近ちかくに＿＿＿つもりだ。

I’m planning to move near the station next month.

引っ越す: to move house.

14. 週しゅう末まつのパーティー、＿＿＿にしてるね！

I’m looking forward to the party this weekend!

楽しみにする: to look forward to something; 楽しみ is the noun.

Home by five — so that nothing happens on the way.

At five o’clock a speaker in the park reminds “good boys and girls” to go home safely; a mother rounds up two children, and one of them argues back. In a few short lines the lesson packs its main grammar point, 〜ように (“so that”), and a handful of everyday spoken forms — なきゃ, なくてもいい, ちゃだめ, でしょ — that you’ll hear far more often than you read them.

This is the first lesson of a JapanesePod101 series in which every dialogue includes a public announcement — the kind you hear in stations, shops and town squares — so it doubles as listening practice. The notes follow the lesson: the scene, its key lines, its vocabulary and its grammar point. Then come a few things around the lesson that are worth knowing for the JLPT, marked Beyond the lesson, and a quiz. The example sentences are mine unless they’re marked as the lesson’s.

The lesson

4 sections
1

The scene

A park, five o’clock, and a speaker on a pole

A mother is at a park with her daughter Misaki and Misaki’s friend Eita when a public announcement comes on: this is Akasaka City Hall. It addresses the children directly — it’s time to go home, and they should be careful on the way so nobody has an accident. The mother tells Eita that it’s five, so it’s time to head home. Eita digs in: he isn’t a “good kid”, he says, so the announcement doesn’t apply to him. The mother tells him not to say that — his parents will worry — and says she and Misaki will walk him home, so it’s time to tidy up. Both children agree, and the toys are put away.

The lesson’s point is listening to announcements. Announcements have their own phrasing — polite, a little formal, addressed to “everyone” — and this one is the kind Japanese children hear every day.

2

Key lines

The lines from the lesson that carry its grammar

Five lines from the lesson’s dialogue, quoted for study. The English is my own rendering.

事故じこに遭あわないように、気きをつけて帰かえりましょう。

Let’s all be careful on the way home, so that nobody has an accident. — the announcement

Grammar: 〜ないように “so as not to” (section 4); 〜ましょう “let’s”; 気をつけて “carefully, taking care”.

英太えいた君くん、５時じだからそろそろお家うちに帰かえらなきゃ。

Eita, it’s five o’clock, so it’s about time you went home. — the mother

Grammar: だから “so”; そろそろ “it’s about time”; 帰らなきゃ = 帰らなければ（いけない） “have to go home” (section 6).

僕ぼくはいい子こじゃないから、帰かえらなくてもいいんだ。

I’m not a good kid, so I don’t have to go home. — Eita

Grammar: じゃないから “because (I’m) not”; 〜なくてもいい “don’t have to”; んだ explaining or insisting. Eita is playing on the announcement’s よい子, “good children”.

そんなこと言いっちゃだめよ。

You mustn’t say things like that. — the mother

Grammar: そんな “that kind of”; 言っちゃだめ = 言ってはだめ “mustn’t say”.

英太えいたくんのパパとママが心配しんぱいするでしょ。

Your mum and dad will worry, you know. — the mother

Grammar: でしょ, a short でしょう, appealing to what the listener already knows. Note she uses his name instead of “your”: Japanese often avoids あなた.

The rest of the scene uses two more patterns worth catching: the mother says she’ll walk him home — お家まで送っていく, literally “send (him) as far as home, going along” — and asks for お片づけ, the gentle word for tidying up that adults use with children.

Vocabulary

The lesson’s ten words, each with a new example

遭あうverb

to run into, meet with (something unwelcome) — an accident, bad weather, trouble

帰かえり道みちで夕立ゆうだちに遭あった。

I got caught in a sudden evening downpour on the way home.

そろそろadverb

it’s about time; soon; before long

そろそろ出でかけましょうか。

Shall we get going?

こちらpronoun

this (polite); this person; this side — and “this is…” on the phone or in announcements

はい、こちらは山田やまだです。

Hello, this is Yamada speaking.

気きを付つけるverb phrase

to be careful, to watch out

階段かいだんが滑すべりやすいので、気きをつけてください。

The stairs are slippery, so please be careful.

片付かたづけnoun

tidying up, putting things away

引ひっ越こしの後あとの片付かたづけに一週間いっしゅうかんかかった。

Sorting everything out after the move took a week.

おもちゃnoun

toy

弟おとうとはなかなかおもちゃを貸かしてくれない。

My little brother hardly ever lends me his toys.

そんなpre-noun

that kind of, such, like that

そんな話はなし、初はじめて聞きいた。

That’s the first I’ve heard of anything like that.

事故じこnoun

accident, incident

駅えきの前まえで事故じこがあったらしい。

Apparently there was an accident in front of the station.

市役所しやくしょnoun

city hall, municipal office

住所じゅうしょが変かわったら、市役所しやくしょで手続てつづきをしてください。

If your address changes, please do the paperwork at city hall.

片付かたづけるる-verb

to tidy up, put away; to deal with, get done

使つかったら、すぐ片付かたづけてね。

Put it away as soon as you’ve used it, OK?

4

〜ように: “so that”

The lesson’s grammar point

〜ように attaches to a verb to give the purpose or aim of an action: “so that…”, or with a negative, “so as not to…”. It comes after:

FormPatternMeaning
Dictionary formわかるように、できるようにso that (someone) can / will…
ない form遭わないように、忘れないようにso that … doesn’t happen

The lesson adds a useful tip: with the dictionary form, ように very often follows a verb that expresses ability or a state — わかる “understand”, できる “be able to”, a potential form like 読める “can read” — because the goal is something you can’t simply do at will. The lesson’s own examples include みんなが分かるように、説明してください, “please explain so that everyone can understand”. Some new ones:

後うしろの人ひとにも聞きこえるように、大おおきい声こえで話はなしてください。

Please speak up so that the people at the back can hear too.

新聞しんぶんが読よめるように、毎日まいにち漢字かんじを十じゅう個こずつ覚おぼえています。

I’m learning ten kanji a day so that I’ll be able to read the newspaper.

遅刻ちこくしないように、目覚めざましを二ふたつかけた。

I set two alarms so that I wouldn’t be late.

風邪かぜをひかないように、暖あたたかくして寝ねてね。

Keep warm in bed so you don’t catch a cold.

Three relatives the lesson points to

毎朝まいあさ水みずを一杯いっぱい飲のむようにしています。

〜ようにする — “make a point of”: I make a habit of drinking a glass of water every morning.

やっと自転車じてんしゃに乗のれるようになった。

〜ようになる — “come to (be able to)”: I can finally ride a bike.

明日あしたは遅おくれないようにしてください。

〜ようにしてください — “please make sure (not) to”: Please make sure you’re not late tomorrow.

Drill — your own reasons

Make three ように sentences about your own study: one with 分かるように, one with a potential verb (話せるように, 読めるように), and one with 忘れないように. Say them aloud, then check the form against the table.

ように or ために?

Beyond the lesson: the pair the JLPT loves to test

Beyond the lessonBoth translate as “so that” or “in order to”, and choosing between them is a classic test question at around N4. The rule of thumb:

ためにように
The goal is…an action you can simply decide to doa state or ability you can only aim for, or something not happening
Typical verbsdictionary form of action verbs: 行く、買う、入るpotential forms, わかる、聞こえる、見える, and 〜ない
Subjectsusually the same person in both halvescan be different people
After nouns健康のために “for my health”not used this way

日本にほんの大学だいがくに入はいるために、毎晩まいばん勉強べんきょうしている。

I study every night in order to get into a Japanese university. (ために: getting in is my decision to pursue, same subject.)

子こどもでも読よめるように、ひらがなで書かいた。

I wrote it in hiragana so that even children can read it. (ように: a potential verb, and the children are a different subject.)

Watch forThe same verb can take either, with a change of meaning. 泳ぐために “in order to swim (somewhere)”, but 泳げるように “so that I’ll be able to swim”. If you can swap in “so that I’ll be able to”, you want ように.

The spoken forms

What the mother and Eita are really saying

The lesson points back to earlier JapanesePod101 lessons for these, so here they are in one place. Each is a shortened, spoken version of a longer textbook form.

SpokenFull formMeaning
帰らなきゃ帰らなければ（いけない）have to go home
帰らなくてもいい—don’t have to go home
言っちゃだめ言ってはだめmustn’t say
心配するでしょ心配するでしょうwill worry, you know / won’t they?
いいんだいいのだ(it’s that) it’s fine — explaining or insisting

Two details trip people up. ちゃ becomes じゃ after verbs whose て-form ends in で: 泳いじゃだめ, 読んじゃだめ. And なきゃ is often left hanging — the “must” part is understood.

もう九時くじだ。宿題しゅくだい、やらなきゃ。

It’s already nine. I’ve got to do my homework.

明日あしたは休やすみだから、早はやく起おきなくてもいいよ。

Tomorrow’s a day off, so you don’t need to get up early.

ここで泳およいじゃだめだよ。危あぶないから。

You mustn’t swim here. It’s dangerous.

外そと、寒さむいでしょ。上着うわぎを着きていって。

It’s cold outside, isn’t it? Wear a jacket.

Watch forThese are spoken and casual. In a JLPT reading passage, or anything you write formally, expect the full forms — 帰らなければならない, 言ってはいけない. In the listening section, expect the short ones.

7

Word notes

遭う, the こんな family, 片付け, and walking someone home

遭う — meeting something you didn’t want

あう is written three ways, and the kanji carries the meaning. The lesson’s point is the third: with 遭, you encounter something bad — an accident, bad weather, a pickpocket, trouble.

会うmeet a person友だちに会う
合うfit, match, agreeサイズが合う
遭うrun into something bad事故に遭う、雨に遭う

旅行中りょこうちゅうにスリに遭あって、財布さいふをなくした。

I had my pocket picked on the trip and lost my wallet.

こんな、そんな、あんな、どんな

The こ・そ・あ・ど family again, this time meaning “this kind of / that kind of / that kind of (over there) / what kind of”. そんな is a short form of そのような. It goes directly before a noun — そんなこと “that sort of thing” — and as そんなに it means “that much”.

どんな音楽おんがくが好すきですか。——そんなに詳くわしくないけど、ジャズかな。

What kind of music do you like? — I don’t know that much about it, but jazz, I suppose.

片付ける → 片付け → お片付け

The verb 片付ける (“tidy up, put away; deal with”) is a る-verb. Drop the る and you get the noun 片付け, “tidying up”. Add the polite お and you get お片付け, the word adults use with small children — exactly as the mother does. The second kanji is often written in hiragana: 片づけ.

送っていく — walking someone home

送る means “send”, but for people it means “see someone off” or “take someone somewhere”. With 〜ていく it becomes “take (you) there, going along with you”.

もう暗くらいから、駅えきまで送おくっていくよ。

It’s dark already, so I’ll walk you to the station.

8

The five o’clock chime

よい子のみなさん, and the town’s loudspeakers

The lesson explains that in residential areas of Japan you hear announcements like this one around five in the evening, through a community wireless system; the hosts add that you don’t hear them in business districts. The greeting よい子のみなさん — よい子 “good children” and みなさん “everyone” — is how you address a group of young children, much like “good boys and girls”. That’s the phrase Eita turns against the announcement.

Beyond the lessonThe speakers belong to each town’s disaster-prevention radio system, 防災行政無線 (ぼうさいぎょうせいむせん), built to warn residents of earthquakes, tsunamis and other emergencies. Many towns use it every evening for a short chime — often the children’s song 「夕焼け小焼け」 (ゆうやけこやけ) — which tells children it’s time to go home and also checks that the speakers work. The time varies by town and often by season: earlier in winter, later in summer. If you live in Japan, your town’s website will list it.

Quiz

Pick the word that fits. Each answer comes with the reason.

1. 日本語のニュースが分わかる＿＿＿、毎日まいにち聞きいています。

I listen every day so that I can understand news in Japanese.

分かる is a state you can’t simply decide to be in, so it takes ように.

2. 留学りゅうがくする＿＿＿、アルバイトでお金かねをためている。

I’m saving money from my part-time job to study abroad.

Studying abroad is an action you choose, and the same person does both things — ために.

3. 大事だいじなことを忘わすれない＿＿＿、メモしておこう。

I’ll make a note so I don’t forget the important things.

After a ない form, use ように: “so as not to”.

4. もう五時ごじだ。そろそろ帰かえら＿＿＿。

It’s five already. I’d better head home.

帰らなきゃ = 帰らなければ: “have to go home”, just like the mother in the lesson.

5. 明日あしたは日曜日にちようびだから、早はやく起おき＿＿＿よ。

Tomorrow’s Sunday, so you don’t need to get up early.

〜なくてもいい: “don’t have to” — Eita’s argument.

6. 図書館としょかんで大おおきい声こえで話はなし＿＿＿。

You mustn’t talk loudly in the library.

話しちゃだめ = 話してはだめ: “mustn’t talk”.

7. 帰かえりに雨あめに＿＿＿、びしょぬれになった。

I got caught in the rain on the way back and was soaked.

Running into something unwelcome, like rain or an accident, is 遭う.

8. 「先生せんせいなんか大嫌だいきらい！」「＿＿＿こと言いわないの。」

“I hate the teacher!” “Don’t say things like that.”

Pointing back at what the other person just said calls for そんな — as the mother does with Eita.

9. 遊あそんだ後あとは、おもちゃを＿＿＿ましょう。

After playing, let’s put the toys away.

片付けましょう: “let’s tidy up”.

10. 「よい子このみなさん」は、誰だれに呼よびかける言葉ことば？

Who is “よい子のみなさん” addressed to?

It addresses young children — roughly “good boys and girls”.

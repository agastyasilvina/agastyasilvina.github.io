A hard-headed boss — and a colleague you can’t repay.

Two old friends, both salarymen, meet for a drink. One is stuck between demanding customers and a stubborn new manager; the other has an assistant so sharp he can never pay her back. Their conversation runs on idioms built from 頭, “head” — 頭が固い, 頭を抱える, 頭が上がらない, 頭が下がる, 頭を冷やす and more — in fast, casual men’s Japanese. The first lesson of a new, much harder series.

The dialogue

The whole scene, retold for review

The lesson’s dialogue, retold in my own wording so you can review it in one place: the same scene, idioms and vocabulary, with the names, the boss and some lines changed. The lesson’s exact key lines are in section 3. Hide the English with the button at the top to test yourself.

Scene

An 居酒屋 after work. Two old friends, Yoshida and Nakano, who work for different companies.

Yoshida

中野なかのと飲のむのも、半年はんとしぶりだな。どうだ、最近さいきん、仕事しごとは？

Half a year since we last had a drink, Nakano. So how’s work lately?

Nakano

それがさ、四月しがつに異動いどうしてきた課長かちょうが頑固がんこで、参まいってるんだよ。

Well — the section chief who transferred in in April is so stubborn, it’s wearing me down.

Yoshida

年としを取とると、誰だれでも頭あたまが固かたくなるもんだよ。

Everyone gets set in their ways as they get older.

Nakano

でも、営えい業ぎょうって、お客きゃくさんの要望ようぼうに柔じゅう軟なんに対応たいおうしなくちゃいけないだろ？

But in sales you have to respond flexibly to what customers want, don’t you?

Nakano

なのに課長かちょうは、ちょっと違ちがうやり方かたをしようとすると、「前例ぜんれいがない」って言いうばかりでさ。

And yet whenever we try doing something a bit differently, all the section chief ever says is “there’s no precedent”.

Yoshida

お客きゃくさんと上司じょうしの板いたばさみか。それは頭あたまが痛いたいな。

Stuck between the customers and your boss, then. That’s a real headache.

Nakano

部署ぶしょのみんなが、課長かちょうには頭あたまを抱かかえてるよ。

The whole department is at its wits’ end with the section chief.

Yoshida

まあ、上司じょうしのことで部下ぶかが苦労くろうするって話はなしは、よく聞きくよな。うちの後輩こうはいも、俺おれのせいで苦労くろうしてるかもしれない。

Well, you often hear about people struggling because of their bosses. My junior might be struggling because of me, too.

Yoshida

後輩こうはいの佐藤さとうさん、頭あたまの回転かいてんがすごく速はやくてさ。まさに一いちを聞きいて十じゅうを知しるタイプなんだ。

Sato, my junior, is incredibly quick-witted — exactly the type who hears one thing and understands ten.

Yoshida

俺おれのミスにもすぐ気きづいてフォローしてくれるし、もう頭あたまが上あがらないよ。

Sato spots my mistakes straight away and covers for me. I owe so much, I can’t say a word.

Nakano

へえ、いい後輩こうはいに恵めぐまれてるんだな。

Wow — you’re lucky to have a junior like that.

Yoshida

いや、正しょう直じき、佐藤さとうさんだって俺おれに不満ふまんはあると思おもうし、頭あたまにくることもあるはずだよ。

Honestly, I’m sure Sato has complaints about me too, and gets fed up with me sometimes.

Yoshida

だから、ありがとうって気持きもちは、毎回まいかいちゃんと言葉ことばにするようにしてるんだ。

That’s why I make a point of saying thank you, properly, every time.

Nakano

そうか……。吉田よしだのそういうところには、頭あたまが下さがるよ。

I see… I take my hat off to you for that, Yoshida.

Nakano

俺おれもちょっと頭あたまを冷ひやして、課長かちょうとうまくやれるように頑張がんばってみるか。

Maybe I should cool my head a bit, and try to make things work with the section chief.

2

The scene

Two friends, two companies, one stubborn manager

This is the first lesson of the series’ fourth season, with a new English host, Chris, alongside Natsuko and Naomi. Its theme is expressions that use 頭, “head”. The dialogue is between two men, Nishimura and Kanda: friends and salarymen at different companies, talking in very casual Japanese — old university friends, perhaps, Naomi guesses.

Over a drink, Kanda complains about the manager who transferred in last month. In sales you have to bend to customers’ demands, but whenever the team tries to handle something specially, the manager says there’s no precedent and won’t sign it off. Nishimura sympathises — caught between customers and boss, what a headache — and talks about his own assistant: brilliant, quick on the uptake, always covering his mistakes. She surely has complaints about him too, but he makes a point of thanking her. Kanda is impressed, and resolves to cool his head and try to get on with the manager.

After the dialogue

Naomi finds it a very serious conversation for a first lesson — drinking with a friend and talking only about work doesn’t sound like much fun. But Natsuko suspects these are things you can’t easily say to people at your own company. And Kanda really does have it hard: he described himself as 「客と上司の板ばさみ」.

板ばさみ, Chris says, could be “caught in a bind”, a “dilemma”, or the less common “between a rock and a hard place”. Here it means Kanda wants to meet his customers’ demands, but his boss says no — he’s trying to satisfy both sides and is stuck in the middle. And it happens all the time: between work and family (“which matters more, me or your job?”), or between love and friendship — falling for a friend’s girlfriend, say. Has Chris? No, Chris says.

Key lines

The lesson’s own lines that carry its idioms

Five lines from the lesson’s dialogue, quoted exactly for study. The English is my own rendering.

誰だれでも、年としとともに、頭あたまが固かたくなるんだよ。

Everyone gets more set in their ways with age. — Nishimura

Grammar: 年とともに, “along with age, as you get older”; 頭が固い, inflexible (section 6); 〜んだよ, explaining.

そうか。客きゃくと上司じょうしの板いたばさみじゃ、頭あたまが痛いたいな。

I see. Stuck between the customers and the boss — that’s a headache. — Nishimura

Grammar: 板ばさみじゃ = 板ばさみでは, “if you’re caught between…” (section 8); 頭が痛い, troubling.

彼女かのじょ、すごく頭あたまの回転かいてんが速はやいんだ。一いちをきいて十じゅうを知しる…というタイプの人ひとだよ。

She’s incredibly quick — the kind of person who hears one thing and understands ten. — Nishimura, on his assistant

Grammar: 頭の回転が速い, quick-witted; 一を聞いて十を知る, catching on from the smallest hint (section 5); 〜というタイプ, “the type who…”.

俺おれのミスもカバーしてくれるし、本当ほんとうに頭あたまが上あがらないよ。

She covers my mistakes too — I really owe her. — Nishimura

Grammar: 〜てくれるし, “she does … for me, and…” (giving reasons with し); 頭が上がらない, so indebted you can’t stand up to someone.

なるほどね。西村にしむらのそういう考かんがえ方かたには、頭あたまが下さがるよ。

I see. I take my hat off to you for thinking that way, Nishimura. — Kanda

Grammar: 〜には頭が下がる, “I deeply admire…”; そういう, “that kind of”.

4

Vocabulary

The dialogue’s working vocabulary, each with a new example

異動いどうnoun · する-verb

transfer to another post or section — 人事異動, a staff reshuffle

来月らいげつから、本社ほんしゃの営えい業ぎょう部ぶに異動いどうになった。

From next month I’m transferred to the sales department at head office.

前例ぜんれいnoun

precedent — 前例がない, “it’s never been done”

前例ぜんれいのないことにも、挑戦ちょうせんしてみたい。

I want to try things that have never been done before.

頑固がんこな-adjective

stubborn, obstinate

祖父そふは頑固がんこで、一度いちど決きめたことは絶対ぜったいに変かえない。

My grandfather is stubborn: once he’s decided something, he never changes it.

柔じゅう軟なんな-adjective

flexible — 柔軟に対応する, “handle something flexibly”

急きゅうな予定よていの変更へんこうにも、柔じゅう軟なんに対応たいおうしてもらえて助たすかった。

They handled the sudden change of plan flexibly, which was a great help.

対応たいおうnoun · する-verb

dealing with, handling, response

お客様きゃくさまからの問とい合あわせに、すぐ対応たいおうした。

I dealt with the customer’s enquiry straight away.

営えい業ぎょうnoun · する-verb

sales (the department or the job); doing business — 営業中, “open”

兄あには自動車じどうしゃ会社がいしゃで営えい業ぎょうをしている。

My older brother works in sales at a car company.

板いたばさみnoun

caught between two sides, in a bind

母ははと妻つまの板いたばさみになって、どうしたらいいかわからない。

Caught between my mother and my wife, I don’t know what to do.

融通ゆうずうが利きくphrase

flexible, accommodating — 融通が利かない, “inflexible, rigid”

あの店みせは融通ゆうずうが利きいて、メニューにない料理りょうりも作つくってくれる。

That restaurant is accommodating — they’ll even make dishes that aren’t on the menu.

恵めぐまれるる-verb

to be blessed with, lucky to have

今年ことしは天気てんきに恵めぐまれて、お祭まつりは大成功だいせいこうだった。

We were blessed with good weather this year, and the festival was a great success.

負おい目めnoun

a feeling of owing someone, indebtedness — 負い目を感じる

助たすけてもらってばかりで、友達ともだちに負おい目めを感かんじている。

My friend is always helping me, and I feel I owe them.

5

異動, 前例, and 一を聞いて十を知る

The lesson’s word notes

異動 — a transfer, not a move

異動 means moving a person — a transfer to another section or post. Moving things, or yourself from place to place, is also いどう, but written 移動, so watch the kanji. Natsuko’s memory trick: personnel 異動 uses 異, as in 異なる, “differ” — moving someone to a different section.

四月しがつの人事じんじで、大阪おおさか支店してんに異動いどうすることになった。

In the April reshuffle, I’m being transferred to the Osaka branch.

会議室かいぎしつまで、椅子いすを移動いどうしてください。

Please move the chairs to the meeting room.

前例 — precedent

As the kanji suggest, 前例 is a “previous example”: something that has happened before, or an established practice. It usually comes as 前例がない or 前例がありません: nothing like this has happened before — the manager’s reason for saying no. Chris’s view: if you keep saying “there’s no precedent”, you can never start anything new.

一を聞いて十を知る — hear one, understand ten

To understand everything from a little explanation: the explanation is the one, the whole is the ten. It means very clever, and it’s always praise, never used in a bad sense. Chris offers “a word to the wise is enough”; the lesson’s own example is 彼は、とても賢くて、一を聞いて十を知るような人だ, “he’s so smart, really quick on the uptake”.

6

The 頭 idioms

The lesson’s focus: “head” as the thinking inside it

頭 is the part of the body above the neck — but it can also mean what you’re thinking inside it. The lesson works through three of the dialogue’s idioms in detail:

頭が固い — English says “hard-headed” too, which makes it easy to remember. It doesn’t mean a literally hard head: it means the thinking inside it is rigid, or old-fashioned — someone who can’t change their opinion, who is 融通が利かない, “inflexible”. Its opposite is 頭が柔らかい, “a soft head”: flexible, open-minded thinking.

頭を抱える — when you think “oh no, what do I do?!”, you grab your head with your hands. That gesture is 頭を抱える, and it means being troubled, at a loss for what to do. Naomi and Natsuko both do the gesture all the time. Is it universal? In the West, Chris says, the similar gesture grabs the cheeks instead — like Munch’s The Scream, Naomi says.

頭が上がらない — used when you owe someone a great deal. When you bow to thank someone, your head goes lower than theirs; you are so grateful, or so indebted, that you can’t raise it again. Whether it’s really gratitude depends on the person, Natsuko says, but it certainly means you feel you owe them: 負い目を感じる. And English’s own head idiom, Chris adds: pigheaded — stubborn, just like 頭が固い.

All ten of the dialogue’s head idioms, with examples of my own:

頭あたまが固かたいidiom

inflexible, set in one’s ways — literally, “a hard head”

父ちちは頭あたまが固かたくて、新あたらしいやり方かたを試ためそうとしない。

My father is so set in his ways, he won’t try anything new.

頭あたまが柔やわらかいidiom

open-minded, flexible in one’s thinking

子こどもは頭あたまが柔やわらかいから、新あたらしいゲームもすぐに覚おぼえる。

Children’s minds are flexible, so they pick up new games in no time.

頭あたまが痛いたいidiom

a headache: troubling, worrying (and literally, a headache)

来月らいげつの引ひっ越こしのことを考かんがえると、頭あたまが痛いたい。

Thinking about next month’s move gives me a headache.

頭あたまを抱かかえるidiom

to be at one’s wits’ end — literally, “to hold one’s head”

急きゅうな出費しゅっぴが重かさなって、頭あたまを抱かかえている。

Unexpected expenses keep piling up, and I’m at my wits’ end.

頭あたまの回転かいてんが速はやいidiom

quick-witted — literally, “the head turns fast”

佐藤さとうさんは頭あたまの回転かいてんが速はやくて、どんな質問しつもんにもすぐ答こたえる。

Sato is quick-witted and answers any question on the spot.

一いちを聞きいて十じゅうを知しるproverb

to understand everything from a hint; quick on the uptake

一いちを聞きいて十じゅうを知しる人ひとと働はたらくと、本当ほんとうに楽らくだ。

Working with someone who catches on from a single hint makes life so much easier.

頭あたまが上あがらないidiom

to owe someone too much to stand up to them — literally, “can’t raise one’s head”

学生がくせい時代じだいにお世話せわになった先輩せんぱいには、今いまでも頭あたまが上あがらない。

I still owe the senior who looked after me at university far too much to argue.

頭あたまにくるidiom

to get angry, to have had enough

何度なんど頼たのんでも約束やくそくを守まもってくれないので、さすがに頭あたまにきた。

They kept breaking their promise however often I asked, and in the end I lost my temper.

頭あたまが下さがるidiom

to be full of admiration — literally, “the head lowers”

毎朝まいあさ道みちを掃除そうじしているボランティアの人ひとたちには、頭あたまが下さがる。

I take my hat off to the volunteers who sweep the streets every morning.

頭あたまを冷ひやすidiom

to cool off, calm down — literally, “cool one’s head”

少すこし頭あたまを冷ひやしてから、もう一度いちど話はなし合あおう。

Let’s cool off a little, then talk it over again.

Easily confused

Beyond the lesson: up, down, and the bow

Beyond the lessonThree of the head idioms look alike, and they make good multiple-choice traps. The difference is whose head moves, and why:

  • 頭が上がらない — your head won’t go up: you owe someone so much that you can’t stand up to them.
  • 頭が下がる — your head goes down by itself: admiration, respect for what someone does.
  • 頭を下げる — you lower your head on purpose: a bow, to thank, to apologise, or to ask a favour.

何度なんども助たすけてもらった先輩せんぱいには、頭あたまが上あがらない。

I owe that senior colleague far too much ever to argue.

毎朝まいあさ五時ごじに起おきて練習れんしゅうを続つづけている姿すがたには、頭あたまが下さがる。

Getting up at five every morning to keep practising — I take my hat off to that.

部長ぶちょうが取引先とりひきさきに頭あたまを下さげて、謝あやまってくれた。

The manager bowed to the client and apologised on our behalf.

Getting angry, and calming down. 頭にくる is the everyday anger of “that really got to me”; 腹が立つ, “the belly rises”, is the plainer, more general “to be angry”; and in casual speech, ムカつく is “that really ticks me off”. Calming down again is the dialogue’s last idiom, 頭を冷やす.

A few more heads worth knowing: 頭を使う, “use your head”; 頭に入れる, “take something in, keep it in mind”; 頭が切れる, “razor-sharp”; 頭が真っ白になる, “your mind goes blank”; and 頭打ち, “hitting a ceiling, levelling off”.

8

Casual men’s talk

Beyond the lesson: decoding two friends at the bar

Beyond the lessonThe dialogue is fast, casual and typical of two men talking to an old friend. Decoded:

In the dialogueWhat it means
俺・お前casual, masculine “I” and “you” — fine between close friends, rude to almost anyone else
飲むの、久しぶりだなぁの turns 飲む into a noun: “drinking with you — it’s been a while”
〜だなぁ・〜よな・〜かもなsentence-final な, casual and male-leaning: musing, seeking agreement, guessing
〜だろう？the casual でしょう？: “don’t you think?”
〜なくちゃいけない= 〜なくてはいけない, “have to”; shorter still, 〜なきゃ (UB lesson 1)
板ばさみじゃじゃ = では: “if you’re caught between…”
苦労するっていうのはっていう = という: “the idea that people struggle”
前例がないとか言ってとか言って, “saying things like…”
伝えるようにしている“I make a point of telling her” (UB lesson 1’s ように)

Watch forDon’t carry 俺 and お前 into polite conversation. At work, or with anyone you don’t know well, use 私 for yourself and the other person’s name, with さん, instead of “you”.

Quiz

Pick the phrase that fits. Each answer comes with the reason.

1. 年としを取とると、誰だれでも頭あたまが＿＿＿なる。

Everyone gets set in their ways with age.

頭が固い: inflexible, set in one’s ways — 頭が固くなる, “become inflexible”.

2. お客きゃくさんと上司じょうしの＿＿＿になって、困こまっている。

I’m stuck between the customers and my boss.

板ばさみ: caught between two sides.

3. 何度なんども助たすけてもらった先輩せんぱいには、＿＿＿。

I owe that senior colleague so much.

頭が上がらない: you owe them too much to stand up to them.

4. 何度なんど言いっても遅刻ちこくするので、さすがに＿＿＿。

They kept being late however often I said something, and I lost my temper.

頭にくる: to get angry. 頭を冷やす is the opposite — calming down.

5. 毎日まいにちボランティアを続つづけている人ひとには、本当ほんとうに＿＿＿。

I truly admire people who volunteer every day.

頭が下がる: admiration. 頭が上がらない is owing someone, not admiring them.

6. 締しめ切きりに間まに合あいそうになくて、＿＿＿いる。

I’m not going to make the deadline, and I’m at my wits’ end.

頭を抱える: at a loss, holding your head in your hands.

7. 少すこし＿＿＿から、もう一度いちど話はなし合あおう。

Let’s cool off a bit, then talk it over again.

頭を冷やす: calm down before trying again.

8. 四月しがつの人事じんじで、大阪おおさか支店してんに＿＿＿になった。

In the April reshuffle I was transferred to the Osaka branch.

Moving a person to another post is 異動, with 異 for “different”; 移動 is moving things, or yourself, from place to place.

9. 彼かれは一いちを聞きいて＿＿＿タイプだ。

He’s the type who catches on from the slightest hint.

一を聞いて十を知る: hear one, understand ten.

10. 質問しつもんにすぐ答こたえられるのは、＿＿＿からだ。

Answering questions straight away comes from being quick-witted.

頭の回転が速い: quick-witted — the head “turns” fast.

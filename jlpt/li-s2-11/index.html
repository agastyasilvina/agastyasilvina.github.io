So we can keep in touch? Wrong number.

Part three of Tom and Junko. Tom asks for Junko’s number so they can keep in touch, saves it, and calls on the spot, but it won’t connect: did she give him a fake number on purpose, so he couldn’t call? The grammar point is 〜ように, “so that”: 連絡できるように, かけられないように. The lesson notes add which verbs come before it, and why its subject can differ from the rest of the sentence.

The dialogue

The whole scene, retold for review

The lesson’s dialogue, retold in my own wording so you can review it in one place. It keeps the same scene, characters, words and grammar, and carries on from my retelling of lesson 10. Every line is rephrased, the number is made up (and can’t exist, as section 7 explains), and the last two lines are new: Tom hopes for the best, and Junko thinks one last thought. The lesson’s exact key lines are in section 3. Hide the English with the button at the top to test yourself.

Scene

The same café. Tom takes out his phone.

Tom

あの、これからも連絡れんらくが取とれるように、携帯けいたいの番号ばんごうを教おしえてもらえませんか。

Um, so that we can stay in touch, could you give me your mobile number?

Junko

えっと……010の、2468の、1357です。

Um… it’s 010, 2468, 1357.

Tom

ありがとうございます。今いま、登録とうろくしました。じゃ、早速さっそくかけてみますね。

Thanks. I’ve just saved it. I’ll give it a try right away, then.

Junko

えっ、今いまですか……？

What, now…?

Tom

あれ？おかしいなあ。何回なんかいかけても、つながらないんですけど。

Huh? That’s odd. However many times I call, it won’t go through.

Tom

もしかして、僕ぼくがかけられないように、わざと偽物にせものの番号ばんごうを教おしえたんじゃないですか？

You didn’t give me a fake number on purpose, did you, so that I couldn’t call?

Junko

まさか、違ちがいますよ。最近さいきん、携帯けいたいの調子ちょうしが悪わるいんです。

Of course not! My phone’s been playing up lately, that’s all.

Junko

じゃあ、メールでやり取とりできるように、アドレスを書かいておきますね。

Here, I’ll write down my email address, so we can write to each other.

Tom

ありがとうございます！今度こんどこそ、ちゃんと届とどくように祈いのります。

Thank you! This time, I’ll pray it actually gets through.

Junko, thinking

（……アドレスも、一文字ひともじだけ違ちがうんだけどね。）

(…The address is one letter off, too, mind you.)

2

The scene

Naomi and Peter, before and after the dialogue

Just the two of us today, Peter announces (ふたりきり). A shame, says Naomi; then we feel the same, says Peter: ひどい. Peter isn’t fond of this series, and not only because of the company: it feels like it’s making fun of Ibaraki. Because Tom speaks Ibaraki dialect, Naomi asks, and says いばらぎ again. いばらき, Peter pleads with everyone: Tom says it wrong, Naomi says it wrong, the voice actor says it wrong. But the voice actor’s accent isn’t strong at all, Naomi says: Naomi’s mother is from Ibaraki, and has a much stronger accent than Tom’s mild one. Peter still doesn’t feel better. The grammar point is ように, “so that”, and the scene is still the café in America, where Tom asked Junko to be his friend. Junko, Naomi notes, looks a bit bothered (迷惑そう).

After the dialogue

What does Naomi make of this way of turning someone down (このふり方)? Naomi likes it: it’s smart. Ever used it? Yes: you give a number with one digit wrong, or an email address with one letter wrong. So it’s a test, Peter asks, passed by anyone who calls anyway? A test nobody can pass, Naomi says, since the information is wrong. People do it quite a lot when someone is being pushy (しつこい); Naomi’s friends do it. Tough on men, says Peter (男がつらいですよ). They won’t notice, Naomi says: they’ll think they misheard. Does Naomi really think men are that stupid, Peter asks: the tenth girl in a row whose number I took down wrong? Still, isn’t a wrong number better than being told to your face? Which is better, Naomi asks: an email that never gets a reply, or one that bounces back from the mailer daemon? Neither is any fun, says Peter. Peter has heard of a special number in New York: whoever calls it is told they have been rejected. A bit sad, says Naomi, but funny.

Phrases worth taking from the chat:

  • ふたりきり — “just the two of us”.
  • お互い同じ気持ちですね — “then we feel the same way”: お互い, “each other, both”.
  • 茨城をバカにしてるような気がします — “it feels like it’s making fun of Ibaraki”: バカにする, and 〜ような気がする, “I get the feeling that…”.
  • このふり方 — “this way of turning someone down”: ふる, to reject or dump, and 〜方, “way of”.
  • 直接言われるよりは、いいでしょ？ — “better than being told to your face, right?”: the passive, and 〜よりは, “rather than”.
3

Key lines

Tom’s request, and his accusation

The lesson’s dialogue is only eight lines long, so just these two are quoted exactly; section 1 covers the rest in my retelling. The English is my own rendering.

ところで、今後こんご連絡れんらくできるように、携帯けいたいの番号ばんごう教おしえてもらえませんか。

By the way, so that I can contact you from now on, could you give me your mobile number? — Tom

Grammar: ところで, “by the way” (lesson 10); 今後, “from now on”; 連絡できるように, a potential + ように, “so that I can get in touch” (section 5); 携帯の番号, with the を dropped, as in speech; 教えてもらえませんか, “could you tell me?”, a polite request.

私わたしが電話でんわをかけられないように、わざと偽物にせものの番号ばんごうを教おしえたんでしょう？

You gave me a fake number on purpose so I couldn’t call you, didn’t you? — Tom

Grammar: 私が…かけられないように, a negative potential + ように, “so that I couldn’t call”, with its own subject, 私が, while it was Junko who gave the number (section 5); わざと, “on purpose”; 偽物の番号, “a fake number”; 〜たんでしょう？, “you did…, didn’t you?”, an accusation.

Vocabulary

The lesson’s word list, in its order

The hosts’ notes first, then a card for each word, plus a few more from the dialogue and the chat, each with an example of my own.

  • 連絡 (れんらく) — junction, communication, connection; getting in touch. 駅に着いたら連絡を下さい, “call me when you get to the station”; 連絡待ってます, “I’ll be waiting to hear from you”, which Peter finds rich coming from someone who hands out wrong numbers. Add 先 and you get 連絡先, contact information: 連絡先を教えて下さい, a little formal. Casually, an email address is a メアド or メルアド.
  • 登録 (とうろく) — registration. 外国人 is a foreigner, so 外国人登録 is foreigner registration, and the card was a 外国人登録証明書, a “Certificate of Alien Registration”, Peter adds. Naomi remembers queueing at the “Aliens” counter entering the United States. Naomi’s example, 日本に90日以上いるの？外国人登録をしなければなりませんよ, is too casual for a customs officer, Peter objects, so Naomi tries again: 日本に90日以上滞在するんですか？外国人登録をして下さいね. (That system has since changed: see section 8.)
  • つながる — to connect: やっとインターネットがつながった, “the internet’s finally working”; 昨日電話したけど、話し中で電話がつながらなかった, “I called yesterday, but the line was busy and I couldn’t get through.”
  • わざと — on purpose: あのボクサーは、わざと試合に負けた, “that boxer lost the fight on purpose.” A fixed match like that is 八百長 (やおちょう), written with the 八百 of 八百屋, a greengrocer’s. Peter has heard it was common in sumo, at least in the past; Naomi thinks of boxing and pro wrestling, which has a script, says Peter. Turning up “fashionably late” is arriving late on purpose, and わざとですか？, “was that on purpose?”, is what Naomi often asks Peter. わざとですよ, Peter replies.
  • 偽物 (にせもの) — a counterfeit, fake, imitation. にせ works as a prefix for anything bogus: もの, a thing, gives a fake object; かね, money, gives にせがね, counterfeit money, though ニセ札, a counterfeit note, is more precise, Naomi says. There’s lots of counterfeit money in Japan, Peter claims, since the notes keep changing; Naomi doubts it, and suspects America has more. America has given up, Peter jokes. Peter sees signs everywhere saying ニセ札禁止, ニセ札お断り; well, nobody puts up ニセ札歓迎, Naomi says. It goes on people too: にせ学生, a fake student; にせ警官, a fake police officer.

Beyond the lessonつながる starts with things joined into one: 電話がつながる, a call goes through; 島と島が橋でつながる, islands linked by a bridge. It also means “to lead to”, a link between cause and result: 努力が成功につながる, “effort leads to success”, or the lesson notes’ 小さいミスが大きい問題につながる, “a small mistake can lead to a big problem”.

Readings to know. 偽物 is usually にせもの, but can be read ぎぶつ; 偽札, a counterfeit note, is にせさつ. Dictionaries mark メアド and メルアド as slang, both short for メールアドレス. And 連絡先 is defined, neatly enough, as the phone number or address you need ために, in order to contact someone.

連絡れんらくnoun · する-verb

contact, getting in touch — 連絡する; 連絡が取れる, “can reach”

着ついたら、必かならず連絡れんらくしてね。

Be sure to let me know when you get there.

連絡先れんらくさきnoun

contact details — 連絡先を教えてください

緊きん急きゅうの連絡先れんらくさきを、ここに書かいてください。

Please write an emergency contact here.

今後こんごnoun · adverb

from now on, in future — 今後ともよろしく

今後こんごとも、よろしくお願ねがいいたします。

I look forward to working with you from here on.

携帯けいたいnoun

mobile phone, short for 携帯電話; also スマホ

携帯けいたいを家いえに忘わすれてきてしまった。

I’ve gone and left my phone at home.

登録とうろくnoun · する-verb

早速さっそくadverb

届とどいた本ほんを、早速さっそく読よみ始はじめた。

I started reading the book as soon as it arrived.

つながるう-verb

山やまの中なかでは、携帯けいたいがつながりにくい。

Up in the mountains, it’s hard to get a signal.

わざとadverb

偽物にせものnoun

調子ちょうしnoun

迷惑めいわくnoun · な-adjective

Making noise late at night is a nuisance to the neighbours.

話はなし中ちゅうnoun

5

〜ように, so that

The grammar point: a verb + ように, then what you do about it

Today’s ように, Peter says, is roughly the English “so that”: a verb before ように, “so that something happens”, then what you do. Naomi’s starters: 電車に間にあうように, “so that we’re in time for the train”; 連絡できるように, “so that I can contact you”, and in full, 連絡できるように、携帯のスイッチをオンにして下さい, “please keep your phone on, so I can reach you.”

1

Plain verb + ように

The verb is in the plain present, the dictionary form, and it says what you want to achieve: 6時の電車に乗れるように, “so that I could catch the six o’clock train”.

2

Then the main clause

What you do about it: 駅まで走った, “I ran to the station”. Or the dialogue’s Eメールで連絡とれるように、メールアドレスを教えますよ. Very useful, Peter says, and very close to ため.

3

Negatives work too

In the plain negative: 遅刻しないように、目覚ましを設定して下さい, “set your alarm so you won’t be late”; 遅刻しないように気を付けて下さい. And the hosts sign off with かぜをひかないように気を付けて下さい: “take care not to catch a cold.”

The lesson notes add three things. The ように clause describes a good or wished-for situation. Its verb is usually a potential or a negative, 〜できる or 〜ない: a state, rather than an action someone chooses. And its subject is often different from the main clause’s: 先生は、みんなが見えるように、黒板に大きな字で書いた, where everyone sees, but the teacher writes; though it can be the same person, as in 私は、風邪を引かないように、毎日しょうがを食べます.

So that……this is what I do
忘わすれないように
so I don’t forget		メモしておく
I write it down
みんなに聞きこえるように
so everyone can hear		大おおきな声こえで話はなす
I speak up
雨あめにぬれないように
so I don’t get wet		傘かさを持もっていく
I take an umbrella
早はやく治なおるように
so it heals quickly		ゆっくり休やすむ
I rest properly

子こどもでも読よめるように、全部ぜんぶの漢字かんじにふりがなをつけた。

I added furigana to every kanji, so that even children could read it.

道みちに迷まよわないように、地図ちずをダウンロードしておいた。

I downloaded a map in advance, so I wouldn’t get lost.

合格ごうかくできるように、神社じんじゃでお守まもりを買かった。

I bought a good-luck charm at the shrine, so that I’d pass.

Watch forWhen the verb after ように is する, it’s lesson 10’s ようにする, “make sure that”: 見えるようにする. Followed by any other verb, it’s today’s “so that”: 見えるように書く.

ように or ために?

Beyond the lesson: Peter’s “very close to ため”, sorted out

Beyond the lessonBoth translate as “so that” or “in order to”, and learners mix them up all the time. The difference is in the verb before them. Upper Beginner S1 #1 has the longer version; in short:

ためにように
The verb before itan action you choose to dosomething that happens: a potential, a negative, an intransitive verb
Who does itthe same person as the main clauseanyone
Example家いえを買かうために貯金ちょきんする家いえが買かえるように貯金ちょきんする

So 家を買うために貯金する is “I’m saving to buy a house”: buying is something you do. 家が買えるように貯金する is “I’m saving so that I’ll be able to buy a house”: being able to isn’t up to you, it’s a state you hope to reach. That’s why the dialogue’s potentials, 連絡できる, かけられない, 連絡取れる, all take ように.

Beyond the lessonThe Japan Foundation’s grammar columns put it the same way: the “so that” ように is mostly preceded by potential forms, intransitive verbs and ない forms, while ために carries the speaker’s will, an active intention. One of its examples: お金を得るように働く sounds wrong, and becomes お金を得ることができるように働く, “work so as to be able to earn money”. Linguists add that with ために, the doer of both clauses is generally the same person.

7

On the phone

Beyond the lesson: why Junko’s number could never work, and what you’ll hear instead

Beyond the lessonJunko’s number could never work. In Japan, 010 is the prefix for calling abroad, so a number that starts 010 is an international call, and the lesson’s 010-1972-1982 would be dialling country code 1, North America, with far too few digits to reach anyone. Japanese mobile numbers have eleven digits and start 070, 080 or 090, with 060 numbers due to join them. (The number in my retelling is just as impossible, on purpose.)

What you’ll hear instead. When a phone is out of range, switched off or in flight mode, au’s network plays おかけになった電話は、電波の届かない場所にある、または電源が入っていないためかかりません: “the phone you are calling is out of range or switched off.” Out of range is 圏外, the word your phone shows when there’s no signal; an engaged line is 話し中.

  • 電話をかける — to make a call; 電話に出る, to answer; 電話を切る, to hang up.
  • 折り返し電話します — “I’ll call you back”: 折り返し, “in return, straight back”.
  • 電波が悪い — “the signal’s bad”; 電池が切れる, “the battery dies”.
  • 着信 — an incoming call; 不在着信, a missed call; 留守電, voicemail.

すみません、今いま電車でんしゃの中なかなので、後あとで折おり返かえし電話でんわします。

Sorry, I’m on the train right now, so I’ll call you back later.

電波でんぱが悪わるくて、よく聞きこえないんだけど……。

The signal’s bad, I can’t really hear you…

8

Fakes, fixes, residence cards

Beyond the lesson: 八百長, ニセ札 and the card that replaced Naomi’s 外国人登録

Beyond the lessonNaomi’s 外国人登録 is history. The lesson dates from 2008. On 9 July 2012, Japan abolished the Alien Registration Act, and the 外国人登録証明書 with it, and brought in a new way of managing residence built around the 在留カード, the residence card. It goes to mid- to long-term residents, not to tourists or anyone staying three months or less, and is issued with landing permission, a change of status or an extension. Once you settle, you register your address at the city or ward office within 14 days, and foreign residents now appear in the same resident register as Japanese citizens.

八百長, the greengrocer’s game. The word is said to come from a greengrocer called 長兵衛, nicknamed 八百長 after 八百屋, who used to play go with a sumo elder. He was good enough to win, but always kept the games level, one win each. Dictionaries tell it as “so it is said”; the earliest recorded use of the word is from 1906.

Sumo’s scandal. Peter’s rumour had a real sequel three years after the lesson. In February 2011, text messages found on phones seized in a police investigation into baseball gambling exposed match-fixing, and the Japan Sumo Association cancelled the March tournament, the first cancellation in 65 years. That April, it ordered twenty people to retire and suspended three more.

Counterfeits, by the numbers. The figures are small: Japanese police found 948 counterfeit notes in 2022, 2,723 in 2024 and 1,408 in 2025. Peter is half right that the notes change to beat counterfeiters, but only about every twenty years: on 3 July 2024, new ¥10,000, ¥5,000 and ¥1,000 notes came out, with Shibusawa Eiichi, Tsuda Umeko and Kitasato Shibasaburō, and a 3D hologram whose portrait turns as you tilt the note, a world first on a banknote. The reason given: printing and computer technology keep improving, so the notes have to stay ahead.

The rejection line. Peter’s New York number was real: the New York City Rejection Line, set up by Jonah Peretti, who later founded BuzzFeed, and the comedian Chelsea Peretti. Whoever called it heard a recording explaining that the person who gave them the number didn’t want to see them again. Similar “rejection hotlines” spread to other American cities.

Quiz

Pick the word or form that fits. Each answer comes with the reason.

1. 後うしろの席せきからも見みえる＿＿＿、大おおきく書かいてください。

Please write it big, so it can be seen from the back seats too.

見える isn’t something anyone chooses to do, so ように, not ために.

2. 日本語にほんごを勉べん強きょうする＿＿＿、日本にほんに来きました。

I came to Japan to study Japanese.

勉強する is a deliberate action, by the same person: ために.

3. 忘わすれ＿＿＿、手てにメモした。

I wrote it on my hand so I wouldn’t forget.

So as not to: 〜ないように.

4. 最さい終しゅう電車でんしゃに間まに合あう＿＿＿、駅えきまで走はしった。

I ran to the station to make the last train.

間に合う, being in time, is an outcome you hope for, not an action: ように.

5. みんなに＿＿＿ように、マイクを使つかった。

I used a microphone so everyone could hear.

Something that happens, not something done on purpose: 聞こえる.

6. 風邪かぜをひかない＿＿＿、気きをつけてね。

Take care not to catch a cold.

〜ないように気をつけて: the hosts’ goodbye.

7. 駅えきに着ついたら、＿＿＿をください。

Get in touch when you reach the station.

連絡をください: let me know, contact me.

8. お名前なまえと＿＿＿を、こちらにお書かきください。

Please write your name and contact details here.

連絡先: contact details. 連絡帳 is a home–school notebook; 連絡網, a phone tree.

9. 新あたらしい番号ばんごうを、携帯けいたいに＿＿＿した。

I saved the new number in my phone.

登録する: to register, or save, a number.

10. 何度なんどかけても、電話でんわが＿＿＿。

However many times I call, it won’t go through.

Intransitive つながる: the call (doesn’t) get through. つなぐ is connecting something yourself.

11. ＿＿＿遅おくれて行いくのが、おしゃれだと思おもっているらしい。

Apparently they think turning up late on purpose is stylish.

わざと: on purpose. Naomi’s “fashionably late”.

12. このブランドの時計とけい、安やすすぎる。＿＿＿じゃない？

This designer watch is far too cheap. Isn’t it a fake?

偽物: a fake; 本物 is the real thing.

13. 電話でんわしたけど、＿＿＿でつながらなかった。

I called, but the line was busy.

話し中: the line is engaged.

14. 最近さいきん、体からだの＿＿＿がいい。

I’ve been feeling in good shape lately.

調子がいい: in good shape, working well.

We appreciate your call — but we’ve closed for the day.

A parcel arrives for Natsu — seal or signature? — and it’s the wrong item. Natsu rings customer service, and gets a recorded message instead: thank you for calling, we’re closed. The lesson reviews the two humble verbs that recorded messages and shop staff live on, いただく and いたす, and the thank-you you give a delivery driver but not your boss: ご苦労様でした.

The whole scene, retold for review

Sound

The intercom: ピンポーン.

Haru

はい。

Yes?

Driver

こんにちは、宅たく配はい便びんです。森川もりかわ春はる様さまに、ナイルからお届とどけ物ものです。

Hello, home delivery. There’s a parcel from Nile for Ms Haru Morikawa.

Haru

あ、はい。今いま、行いきます。

Oh, yes — coming now.

Driver

お手数てすうですが、こちらに認みとめ印いんをいただけますか。

Sorry to trouble you — could I have your seal here?

Haru

すみません、サインでもいいですか。

はい、大丈夫だいじょうぶです。……ありがとうございました。

Huh? This isn’t what I asked for… I’ll get in touch with them.

2

The scene

A delivery, the wrong item, and an answering machine

The intercom goes. It’s a home-delivery driver with a parcel for Natsu Andō from “Mamazon” — the lesson’s joke on a certain online shop. Could Natsu stamp a personal seal here? Natsu asks to sign instead, which is fine, and thanks the driver: ご苦労様でした. Then — this isn’t what was ordered. Natsu calls the customer service centre, and hears a recorded message: thank you for your call; this is Mamazon’s customer service centre; we appreciate you taking the trouble to call, but we’ve closed for the day; our hours are 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays. Thank you for calling.

After the dialogue

The shop had closed, so Natsu got an answering machine, Tomoyuki notes. Do the hosts shop online? Jessi sometimes does — books can be cheaper online than in shops — and Tomoyuki makes a point of buying online when it’s cheaper. Tomoyuki has parcels delivered all the time, and uses a 認め印, a personal seal, to sign for them whenever possible.

Does Jessi have a seal, a ハンコ? Yes — with ジェシー on it, in katakana. Japan has lots of seal shops, Tomoyuki says, selling everything from cheap to expensive ones, so having one made could be a good souvenir of a trip to Japan.

Key lines

The lesson’s own lines that carry its grammar

Five lines from the lesson’s dialogue, quoted exactly for study. The English is my own rendering.

安藤あんどう夏なつ様さまにママゾンからお届とどけ物ものです。

There’s a parcel from Mamazon for Ms Natsu Andō. — the driver

Grammar: 様, the formal さん used to customers; お届け物, “a delivery”, with the polite お.

こちらに、認みとめ印いんをいただけますか？

Could I have your seal here, please? — the driver

Grammar: こちら, the polite ここ; いただけますか, “could I humbly receive…?” — the potential of いただく (section 6).

ご苦く労ろう様さまでした。あれ？注ちゅう文もんしたものじゃない。

Thanks for your trouble. … Huh? This isn’t what I ordered. — Natsu

Grammar: ご苦労様でした (section 5); 注文したもの, a clause describing もの: “the thing I ordered”; じゃない, “isn’t”.

せっかくお電話でんわをいただきましたが、本日ほんじつの営えい業ぎょうは終しゅう了りょういたしました。

We appreciate you taking the trouble to call, but we have closed for the day. — the recording

Grammar: せっかく, “after all your trouble”; いただきました, humble “received”; 終了いたしました, humble “has finished” (section 6).

営えい業ぎょう時間じかんは、平日へいじつ午前ごぜん10時じから午後ごご6時じまでです。

Our business hours are 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays. — the recording

Grammar: 営業時間, “business hours”; 平日, “weekdays”; 〜から〜まで, “from … until …”.

Vocabulary

The lesson’s five words, then five more from the dialogue, each with a new example

いただくう-verb

to receive (humble もらう); also to eat or drink, humbly

先生せんせいから、旅行りょこうのお土産みやげをいただいた。

My teacher gave me a souvenir from a trip.

せっかくadverb

with effort, specially; long-awaited — せっかく〜のに, “all that trouble for nothing”

せっかく早起はやおきしたのに、雨あめで遠足えんそくが中止ちゅうしになった。

I got up early specially, and the school trip was called off for rain.

いたすう-verb

to do (humble する)

ご注ちゅう文もんの品しなは、明日あしたお届とどけいたします。

We will deliver your order tomorrow.

認みとめ印いんnoun

an everyday personal seal, not officially registered

書留かきとめを受うけ取とるとき、認みとめ印いんを押おした。

I stamped my seal when I signed for the registered mail.

ご苦く労ろう様さまset phrase

thank you for your trouble — to someone who has worked for you

荷物にもつを運はこんでくれた人ひとに、「ご苦く労ろう様さまでした」と言いった。

I said “thank you for your trouble” to the person who carried the parcel.

宅たく配はい便びんnoun

home delivery service; a parcel sent that way

重おもいスーツケースは、宅たく配はい便びんで空港くうこうに送おくった。

I sent my heavy suitcase to the airport by home delivery.

お届とどけ物ものnoun

a delivery, something delivered — 届ける, “deliver”

留守るすの間あいだに、お届とどけ物ものが来きていた。

A delivery came while I was out.

注ちゅう文もんnoun · する-verb

an order — 注文する, “to order”

ネットで注ちゅう文もんした本ほんが、もう届とどいた。

The book I ordered online has already arrived.

営えい業ぎょう時間じかんnoun

business hours, opening hours

この店みせの営えい業ぎょう時間じかんは、夜よる九時くじまでです。

This shop is open until 9 p.m.

平日へいじつnoun

a weekday

平日へいじつは忙いそがしいので、週末しゅうまつはゆっくり休やすみたい。

Weekdays are busy, so I want to take it easy at the weekend.

5

ご苦労様 or お疲れ様?

The lesson’s phrase note: two ways to thank someone for their effort

苦労 means trouble or hardship, so ご苦労様でした is literally “that was hard work” — but it’s used to thank someone for their effort. It’s similar to お疲れ様でした, “thank you for your hard work”. The difference, Tomoyuki says, is that you’re told not to say ご苦労様 to people above you. In the dialogue it’s fine: a customer is saying it to the person delivering their parcel. At work, though, the hosts recommend お疲れ様でした or お疲れ様です.

PhraseWho says it to whom
ご苦労様です・でしたto someone who has done work for you: a delivery driver, a junior, someone who’s run an errand
お疲れ様です・でしたto anyone at work, including your boss: in passing, at the end of the day, after a meeting

重おもい荷物にもつを運はこんでくれた人ひとに、「ご苦く労ろう様さまです」と声こえをかけた。

I said “thank you for your trouble” to the person who carried in the heavy parcel.

お疲つかれ様さまです。会議かいぎの資料しりょう、ここに置おいておきますね。

Hi, thanks for all your work — I’ll leave the meeting documents here, OK? (To a colleague or a boss.)

Watch forThe “never to superiors” rule is younger than it sounds. In the Edo period 御苦労 was said to superiors too; the restriction spread through business-manners and keigo books from the 1970s, and one researcher calls it a myth passed down inside companies (Kuramochi, 2011). But it is now what most people go by. In the Agency for Cultural Affairs’ 2024 survey, 77% said お疲れ様 to a superior after shared work, and just 3.5% ご苦労様. To a delivery person of their own age, 70% simply said ありがとう, and 20% ご苦労様 — more often the older they were. So: お疲れ様 at work, ありがとうございます anywhere, and ご苦労様 to the driver is fine.

6

いただく and いたす

The lesson’s grammar point: two humble verbs

The lesson’s focus is listening to an automated phone system — and the two humble verbs it is built from, which appear in one sentence: お電話をいただきましたが、本日の営業は終了いたしました.

Plain verbHumble verb, and its ます-form
もらう “receive”いただく → いただきます
する “do”いたす → いたします

いただく, the humble “receive”

いただく is the humble form of もらう. プレゼントをもらいます is already formal; replace もらいます with いただきます and it becomes extra formal. The lesson’s example: 社長からプレゼントをいただきました, “I received a present from the company president.” And you already know it in a set phrase: いただきます before a meal means, literally, “I humbly receive (this food)”.

先生せんせいから、すてきなお土産みやげをいただいた。

My teacher gave me a lovely souvenir.

もう少すこしゆっくり話はなしていただけますか。

Could you speak a little more slowly, please? (〜ていただけますか: “could I humbly receive your…ing?” — the driver’s polite request.)

いたす, the humble “do”

いたす is the humble form of する, and its ます-form is いたします. 失礼します, “excuse me”, becomes even more formal as 失礼いたします.

明日あした、こちらからご連絡れんらくいたします。

We will contact you tomorrow.

The whole sentence, piece by piece

お電話を “your phone call” · いただきました “(we) received” · が “but” · 本日の “today’s” · 営業は “business” · 終了いたしました “has finished”. Literally, “we humbly received your call, but today’s business has finished” — or, in natural English, “we appreciate your call, but we’ve closed for the day”.

Drill — make it humble

Rewrite each with いただく or いたす: (a) 先輩に本をもらった。 (b) 私が案内します。 (c) ここにサインをもらえますか。

Answers

(a) 先輩に本をいただいた。 (b) 私がご案内いたします。 — 案内 takes the polite ご too. (c) ここにサインをいただけますか。

The keigo map

Beyond the lesson: where いただく and いたす sit, and their partners

Beyond the lessonJapan’s official keigo guidelines, the 敬語の指針 (2007), put the lesson’s two verbs in different boxes. いただく is 謙譲語Ⅰ: it lowers you in order to raise the person you receive from — here, the caller. いたす is 謙譲語Ⅱ, or 丁重語, like lesson 3’s おります: it raises no one in the sentence and is simply courtesy to the listener — which is how a recording can use it about a shop’s own hours, 本日の営業は終了いたしました.

Each has a partner, for when the other person is the subject:

You are the subject (humble)They are the subject (honorific)
いただく ← もらうくださる ← くれる, “give me”
〜ていただけますか〜てくださいますか: both “would you…?”
いたす ← するなさる ← する, “do”

先生せんせいが本ほんをくださった。先生せんせいに本ほんをいただいた。

My teacher gave me a book. I received a book from my teacher. (The same event: くださる makes the teacher the subject; いただく makes it you.)

お〜いたします. Put お or ご in front of a verb’s stem or a する-noun, add いたします, and you have the commonest humble pattern in shops and offices: お届けいたします, ご案内いたします. The guidelines count it as both kinds of humble at once.

させていただく. You’ll hear it everywhere. The guidelines say it fits when you do something with the other person’s permission and feel it as a favour — コピーを取らせていただけますか, “may I take a copy?” Where no permission is involved, plainer is better: 休業いたします rather than 休業させていただきます for “we are closed”.

And せっかく, three ways (デジタル大辞泉): せっかく来てくれたんだから, “since you went to the trouble of coming”; せっかくの休日, “a precious day off”; and せっかく努力したのに, “I worked so hard — for nothing”. The recording’s せっかくお電話をいただきましたが is the first kind, turned into an apology — and せっかくですが is the polite way to turn down an offer.

8

Seals and deliveries

Beyond the lesson: ハンコ, signatures, and parcels in Japan

Beyond the lessonThree kinds of seal. A 実印 is registered with your city or ward office, which can issue a certificate, the 印鑑登録証明書, to prove it’s yours — needed for property, cars and big contracts. A 銀行印 is the one registered with your bank. A 認め印 is any other personal seal: the everyday one for parcels and office forms (デジタル大辞泉). Seals are fading from paperwork, though. In November 2020 the government announced that 14,909 of the 14,992 administrative procedures requiring one — over 99% — would drop the requirement; the 83 kept involve registered seals, registrations and banks (Cabinet Office, 2020).

Seal or signature? Either works, as Natsu found. Japan Post’s missed-delivery slips have asked for a “seal (or signature)” since 2014; Yamato, which stopped asking for either during the pandemic, began asking for a seal or signature again on 1 August 2026; and Sagawa now uses an electronic signature or a code for some parcels (Yamato; Japan Post).

Not at home? The driver leaves a missed-delivery slip, a 不在連絡票, and you book a 再配達, a redelivery. In April 2026 about 7.6% of parcels needed one, by the transport ministry’s twice-yearly count (MLIT, 2026). Cutting that matters because of the “2024 problem”, 物流の2024年問題: since April 2024, truck drivers’ overtime has been capped at 960 hours a year, and without changes, transport capacity was projected to fall about 14% short (MLIT). So the government is pushing ways to receive parcels with no one at home — 置き配, left at your door; delivery lockers; convenience-store pickup — which covered about 31% of the three biggest carriers’ parcels in April 2026, against a target of about half by 2030.

再配達さいはいたつをお願ねがいしたいんですが。

I’d like to arrange a redelivery, please.

玄関げんかんの前まえに置おいておいてください。

Please leave it outside the front door.

Quiz

Pick the word that fits. Each answer comes with the reason.

1. 社長しゃちょうから記念品きねんひんを＿＿＿。

I received a commemorative gift from the company president.

Receiving from someone above you: humble もらう, いただきました. くださいました would need the president as the subject — 社長がくださいました.

2. 明日あした、こちらからご連絡れんらく＿＿＿。

We will contact you tomorrow.

Your own action, humbly: する → いたす. なさる is honorific, for the other person’s actions.

3. こちらにサインを＿＿＿。

Could I have your signature here?

いただけますか, “could I humbly receive…?”, is the driver’s polite request.

4. （配達はいたつの人ひとに）「＿＿＿でした。」

(To the delivery person) Thank you for your trouble.

ご苦労様でした thanks someone for work done for you — fine from a customer to a delivery driver.

5. （会社かいしゃで、部長ぶちょうに）「＿＿＿です。」

(At work, to your manager) Thanks for all your work.

To someone above you, the safe choice is お疲れ様です; ご苦労様 is taught as being for people below you.

6. （部屋へやに入はいるとき）「＿＿＿。」

(Going into a room) Excuse me.

失礼します made humbler: する → いたす.

7. ＿＿＿来きてくれたのに、留守るすにしていてごめんね。

You came all this way and I wasn’t in — sorry.

せっかく〜のに: you went to the trouble, and it came to nothing.

8. 荷物にもつを受うけ取とるとき、＿＿＿を押おした。

I stamped my seal when I took in the parcel.

認め印: an everyday, unregistered personal seal.

9. 営えい業ぎょう時間じかんは、＿＿＿の午前ごぜん十時じゅうじから午後ごご六時ろくじまでです。

Business hours are 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays.

平日: weekdays, Monday to Friday.

10. （食たべる前まえに）「＿＿＿。」

(Before eating)

いただきます: literally, “I humbly receive (this food)”.

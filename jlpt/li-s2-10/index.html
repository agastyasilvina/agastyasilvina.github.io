One new word, every day.

Part two of Tom and Junko. Junko asks how Tom got so good at Japanese, and Tom lists his habits: at least one new word a day, Japanese anime and films in his free time, favourite lines copied into a notebook, and a chat with every cute Japanese girl he spots. The grammar point is 〜ようにする, above all 〜ようにしている, “to make a point of doing”: an effort kept up over time. The lesson notes add its second use, “to make sure that”: みんながリラックスできるようにした.

The dialogue

The whole scene, retold for review

The lesson’s dialogue, retold in my own wording so you can review it in one place. It keeps the same scene, characters, words and grammar, and carries on from my retelling of lesson 9. Every line is rephrased, and the last three are new: Junko works out why Tom spoke to her, and hands him a ようにしてください of her own. The lesson’s exact key lines are in section 3. Hide the English with the button at the top to test yourself.

Scene

The same café, a few minutes later. Junko has decided to stay and talk for a while.

Junko

ところで、トムさんの日本語にほんご、どうしてそんなに上手じょうずなんですか。

By the way, Tom, how is your Japanese so good?

Tom

毎日まいにち、新あたらしい単語たんごを少すくなくとも一ひとつは覚おぼえるようにしているんです。

Every day, I make a point of learning at least one new word.

Tom

暇ひまな時ときは、日本にほんのアニメやドラマを見みるようにしています。

In my free time, I make a point of watching Japanese anime and TV dramas.

Tom

気きに入いったセリフがあったら、すぐノートに書かき写うつすようにしているんですよ。

And when there’s a line I like, I make a point of copying it straight into a notebook.

Junko

へえ、意外いがいと勉べん強きょう熱心ねっしんなんですね。

Wow, you’re more studious than you look.

Tom

それから、町まちでかわいい日本人にほんじんを見みかけたら、必かならず話はなしかけるようにしています。

And whenever I see a cute Japanese girl in town, I make a point of talking to her.

Junko

……ああ、だから私わたしにも。

…Ah, so that’s why you talked to me.

Tom

えっ、いや、それは、その……。

Huh? No, that’s, um…

Junko

ふふ。これからは、相手あいての気持きもちも考かんがえるようにしてくださいね。

Heh. From now on, please make a point of thinking about how the other person feels, too.

2

The scene

Naomi, Yuichi and Peter, before and after the dialogue

Yuichi is back: お帰りなさい, ただいま. Yuichi went to Korea last week, which Peter hadn’t even noticed: ひどいです, says Yuichi. The grammar point is 〜ようにする: doing something, or trying to, habitually. Since Yuichi missed part one, Naomi recaps: there are two characters, Tom, a university student majoring in Japanese, probably American (judging by the name, 名前からして, says Yuichi), and Junko, who is Japanese. Tom’s voice actor gave him an Ibaraki or Tochigi accent. Last week, Tom spoke to Junko in a café and asked her to be his friend.

After the dialogue

What does Yuichi make of Tom talking to every cute Japanese girl he finds? Hard in Japan, Yuichi thinks: Japanese girls are shy, Naomi adds, so it’s difficult to talk to someone you don’t know. If you really want to, Yuichi suggests, ask a question, honestly: 日本語教えて下さい. Too sudden, Naomi says, but asking a question does work with Japanese women, even just asking the way. Peter often says it’s hard to pay Japanese women a compliment, Naomi goes on: they turn red and say no, and an unnatural compliment makes a colleague, Sakura, angry. They suspect Peter of being after something (何ねらっている？): Peter is あやしい, “dodgy”, Naomi says. Isn’t it down to looks and what you say, Peter asks? Both matter, Yuichi agrees. If Brad Pitt spoke to Naomi, Peter says, anything would do, and Naomi agrees: Japanese lessons, free of charge (ただで教える).

Phrases worth taking from the chat:

  • 韓国に行ってきたんですよね — “you’ve been to Korea, haven’t you?”: lesson 6’s 行ってくる.
  • 名前からして — “judging by the name”: 〜からして.
  • 何かを質問すればいいと思います — “I think you should just ask a question”: 〜ばいい, advice.
  • 何ねらっている？ — “what are you after?”: ねらう, to aim at.
  • ただで教える — “I’ll teach for free”: ただ, free of charge.
3

Key lines

Two of Tom’s habits, in the lesson’s words

The lesson’s dialogue is only seven lines long, so just these two are quoted exactly; section 1 covers the rest in my retelling. The English is my own rendering.

毎日まいにち、一日いちにち一個いっこは新あたらしい単語たんごを覚おぼえるようにしています。

I make a point of learning at least one new word a day. — Tom

Grammar: 一日一個は, “at least one a day”: は after a number sets a minimum; 覚える, to memorise (section 4); 〜ようにしています, a habit kept up (section 5).

アニメや映画えいがを見みるときは、気きに入いったセリフをノートに書かくようにしています。

When I watch anime or films, I make a point of writing my favourite lines in a notebook. — Tom

Grammar: 〜や〜, “… and … and so on”; 見るときは, “whenever I watch”; 気に入ったセリフ, “lines I like”: 気に入る, read きにいる, and セリフ, a line (section 4); 書くようにしています (section 5).

4

Vocabulary

The lesson’s word list, in its order

The hosts’ notes first, then a card for each word, plus a few more from the dialogue and the chat, each with an example of my own.

  • ところで — by the way: for changing the subject and starting something new. Yuichi’s examples: ところで、お昼ごはん何食べますか？ and ところで、今夜ひまですか？, “are you free tonight?”. A good pick-up line, Naomi says, though Yuichi warns that you need a long conversation first. Combine their advice, Peter jokes, and you get 日本語教えて、ところで、今夜ひまですか？
  • 覚える — to memorise, remember, recall. The difference between memorise and remember is hard for Japanese speakers, Naomi says. Peter always struggled with “make me remember”, and ended up with 思い出させて; a perfect translation, Naomi says, but not something people say much. 思い出す is a little different: 覚える is close to memorise, and 覚えている to remember. 単語を覚える, “to memorise a word”; この単語何？覚えてないな, “what’s this word? I don’t remember.”
  • 暇 (ひま) — free time, leisure. Naomi and Yuichi bring in the proverb 貧乏ひまなし, “there’s no leisure for the poor”: poor people have no time to spare.
  • 気に入る — to like, take a fancy to. It’s read きにいる, not きにはいる, Naomi warns; the negative is 気に入らない. お気に入り is a favourite, and it’s used a lot. Peter declares Yuichi the favourite intern (Internの中で、Yuichiくんが、僕のお気に入り), but Naomi warns that Peter tells Kiyoko the same, and Miki, a part-timer, would win anyway. Peter makes Yuichi practise 気に入ってくれてありがとうございます, “thank you for liking it”, which comes out with no feeling at all. Naomi adds 気に入ってもらってうれしいです, “I’m glad you like it”.
  • セリフ — a line, as in a play. Written 台詞, it isn’t read だいし: it’s an irregular reading, so the word is often written in katakana or hiragana. 台 is probably the stage, Naomi thinks, and 詞 the words: the words you speak on stage. セリフを忘れる, to forget your lines; Yuichi’s あの役者はステージでセリフを忘れた. Peter often hears こっちのセリフ: “that’s what I want to say”, “same here”. Yuichi tries it out: 僕のお気に入りはPeterさんです; こっちのセリフ.
  • 意外 (いがい) — unexpected, surprising. 意 is the mind and 外 outside, Yuichi explains: outside what you had in mind. It’s a な-adjective, 意外な人, and with a particle an adverb: 意外に or 意外と, which work the same. チョコレートは意外と高い, “chocolate is surprisingly expensive”.
  • 熱心 (ねっしん) — zeal, enthusiasm. 勉強熱心 is studious, eager to learn; 仕事熱心, hard-working, devoted to the job. Naomi is a 熱心な先生, Peter says; 仕事熱心, Yuichi agrees; sometimes, Peter adds. What do you want from me, Naomi asks? Five thousand yen, maybe. And Naomi’s line for the end: Japanesepod101は、熱心なListenerに支えられています.

Beyond the lesson覚える or 覚えている? The dictionary gives 覚える two senses: keeping something in mind, and learning it, getting it into your head. For the state of still having it, Japanese uses 〜ている: 子どものころのことは覚えていない, “I don’t remember my childhood”. 思い出す is the moment it comes back to you.

意外と or 意外に? Both mean “surprisingly”. One dictionary still marks 意外と as colloquial, while another notes it is now used just like 意外に: in conversation, either is fine.

こっちのセリフ has a dictionary entry of its own: the words you should be saying, thrown straight back at the other person, as in 「出て行け」はこっちのセリフだ, “‘Get out’? That’s my line.”

ところでconjunction

by the way — to change the subject

ところで、来らい週しゅうの会議かいぎは何時なんじからでしたっけ。

By the way, what time does next week’s meeting start again?

覚おぼえるる-verb

to memorise, learn; 覚えている, “to remember”

新あたらしい同どう僚りょうの名前なまえが、なかなか覚おぼえられない。

I just can’t get my new colleagues’ names into my head.

暇ひまな-adjective · noun

free time; free, not busy — 暇な時; 貧乏暇なし

暇ひまな時ときは、近ちかくの公園こうえんを散歩さんぽする。

When I have time, I go for a walk in the park nearby.

気きに入いるう-verb

to like, take to — read きにいる; 気に入らない, “not to like”

このかばん、色いろも形かたちもすごく気きに入いっている。

I really love this bag, the colour and the shape.

お気きに入いりnoun

a favourite — お気に入りの店, “a favourite shop”

この曲きょくが、最近さいきんのお気きに入いりだ。

This song is my favourite at the moment.

セリフnoun

a line in a play or film — also 台詞; こっちのセリフ, “that’s my line”

主役しゅやくの最後さいごのセリフに、思おもわず泣ないてしまった。

The lead’s last line made me cry in spite of myself.

意外いがいな-adjective · noun

unexpected, surprising — 意外に, 意外と, “surprisingly”

この店みせのカレーは、意外いがいと辛からい。

The curry here is surprisingly hot.

熱心ねっしんな-adjective

keen, devoted — 勉強熱心, studious; 仕事熱心, hard-working

父ちちは仕事しごと熱心ねっしんで、休やすみの日ひもパソコンに向むかっている。

My father is so devoted to his work he’s at the computer even on days off.

単語たんごnoun

a word, a vocabulary item — 単語帳, “word book”

毎朝まいあさ、電車でんしゃの中なかで英語えいごの単語たんごを覚おぼえている。

Every morning I learn English words on the train.

話はなしかけるる-verb

to speak to, start a conversation with

隣となりの席せきの人ひとに、思おもい切きって話はなしかけてみた。

I plucked up the courage to talk to the person in the next seat.

一いっ生しょう懸けん命めいな-adjective · adverb

with all your might, as hard as you can

試験しけんに向むけて、一いっ生しょう懸けん命めい勉べん強きょうしている。

I’m studying as hard as I can for the exam.

(辞書じしょを)引ひくう-verb

to look up (in a dictionary) — 辞書を引く; Peter read his: 辞書を読む

分わからない言葉ことばは、すぐに辞書じしょを引ひくようにしている。

When I meet a word I don’t know, I make a point of looking it up straight away.

怪あやしいい-adjective

suspicious, dodgy, fishy — what Naomi calls Peter

そんなにうまい話はなしは、ちょっと怪あやしいね。

A deal that good sounds a bit fishy.

ただnoun

free (of charge) — ただで, “for free”

この美術館びじゅつかんは、子こどもはただで入はいれる。

Children get into this museum for free.

5

〜ようにしている

The grammar point: making a point of doing something, over time

〜ようにする has two uses, Peter explains: someone brings about a change in their own behaviour, trying to do something; or someone brings about a change in a situation. This lesson covers the first. Naomi’s example from the dialogue: 日本のアニメや映画を見るようにしています, “I make a point of watching Japanese anime and films”.

1

日本のアニメや映画を

Japanese anime や films: や lists examples, “anime, films and so on”. Then the object marker を.

2

見る + ようにしています

The verb before ようにする is in the plain non-past, the dictionary form: 見るようにする, 食べるようにする. Naomi’s: Healthyなものを食べるようにしている, “I’m making a point of eating healthily.”

3

Not the same as てみる

〜てみる also translates as “try”, Peter warns, but it’s trying something once. ようにする is habitual, an ongoing effort over a period of time: buying healthy food every day, fighting the urge to eat sweets.

And the hosts’ own habits. Learning English, Yuichi たくさん読むようにしました, made a point of reading a lot. Naomi 映画の字幕を読むようにしました, and still does, in English: 今も英語の字幕を読むようにしています. Peter, who studied Japanese in Japan: 一生懸命漢字を覚えるようにしていました. How? Not newspapers, not writing: Peter carried a small pocket kanji dictionary and 毎日辞書を読むようにしていました, read it every day. Read it, Yuichi marvels, not just look things up (辞書を引く). 熱心に勉強したんですね; long ago, says Peter.

The lesson notes add that for a habit, the progressive 〜ようにしている is the usual form, and that the verb before it must be something you can choose to do. In practice, the forms divide up like this:

FormWhat it says
〜ようにするI’ll make a point of it, from now on: a resolution
〜ようにしているI make a point of it: a habit you keep up
〜ようにしたI made a point of it, or started to
〜ないようにするI make a point of not doing it
〜ようにしてくださいplease make sure you do: a polite instruction

寝ねる前まえは、なるべくスマホを見みないようにしている。

Before bed, I make a point of not looking at my phone, as far as I can.

これからは、毎朝まいあさ10分ぷんだけでも日本語にほんごを聞きくようにします。

From now on, I’ll make sure I listen to Japanese every morning, even for just ten minutes.

健康けんこうのために、エレベーターではなく階段かいだんを使つかうようにしている。

For my health, I make a point of taking the stairs, not the lift.

会議かいぎには遅おくれないようにしてください。

Please make sure you’re not late for the meeting.

6

〜ようにする, making sure

The lesson notes’ second use: changing a situation so that something can happen

The hosts leave it for another day, but the lesson notes explain the second use: someone changes a situation so that something can happen. Here the verb before ように has its own subject, marked with が, and it’s a different subject from the sentence’s. The notes’ examples: 私は、みんながリラックスできるようにした, “I made sure everyone could relax”, and 私は、ドアが開くようにした, “I made sure the door would open”. The shape:

1

[subject]は

Who makes the change: 私は.

2

[someone or something]が + plain verb

What should become possible: みんながリラックスできる, ドアが開く. Often a potential form, or an intransitive verb.

3

ようにする

“Make it so that”: arrange things so that it can happen.

窓まどを開あけて、風かぜが通とおるようにした。

I opened the windows so the air could flow through.

先生せんせいは、全員ぜんいんが発言はつげんできるようにしてくれた。

The teacher made sure everyone got a chance to speak.

後うしろの席せきからも見みえるように、字じを大おおきく書かいた。

I wrote in big letters so it could be seen from the back seats too.

Watch forThe last example is plain ように, “so that”, from Upper Beginner S1 #1: here it’s followed by another verb (書いた) instead of する. The two are close cousins: 見えるようにする is “make it visible”; 見えるように書く, “write so that it’s visible”.

The ように family

Beyond the lesson: every ように so far, and its look-alikes

Beyond the lessonLessons 9 and 10 teach a pair, and earlier lessons have used their relatives. Side by side, with an example of each:

PatternMeaning, with an example
〜ようになるa change that happens (lesson 9): 泳およげるようになった, I learned to swim
〜ようにするa change you work at: 毎日まいにち歩あるくようにする, I’ll make a point of walking every day
〜ようにしているa habit kept up: 早はやく寝ねるようにしている, I make a point of going to bed early
〜ように + verbso that (UB S1 #1): 忘わすれないようにメモする, I note it down so I don’t forget
〜ように言いうtell someone to: 早はやく来くるように言いわれた, I was told to come early
〜てみるtry it once: 一度いちど食たべてみる, I’ll try it once
〜ことにするdecide to: 毎朝まいあさ走はしることにした, I’ve decided to run every morning

The last pair is easy to mix up with ようにする. ことにした reports a decision; ようにしている, the effort of sticking to it. You might well say both: 毎朝走ることにした。雨の日も走るようにしている, “I decided to run every morning, and I make a point of running even when it rains.”

8

Word stories

Beyond the lesson: where 気に入る, 台詞, 一生懸命 and their friends come from

Beyond the lessonWhy 気に入る isn’t きにはいる. いる is the old reading of 入る. Modern Japanese says はいる, which began as 這い入る, “to crawl in”, but the older いる survives in set phrases, 気に入る and 堂に入る, “to be a past master at”, and after other verbs: 寝入る, to fall fast asleep; 恐れ入る, to be deeply grateful or overawed.

台詞 is a spelling you simply have to know: it isn’t on the official list of special readings, and in the jōyō table 詞 is only read シ. The word せりふ spread into everyday use from Noh and Kyōgen. Edo-period writers spelled it all sorts of ways, and it was the Meiji dictionary 言海 that settled on 台詞, a word Chinese uses too. Naomi’s “stage plus words” makes a good way to remember it.

From 一所懸命 to 一生懸命. Peter’s 一生懸命, “with all your might”, began as 一所懸命: in medieval Japan, warriors staked their lives on the one place, the piece of land, they held. Once the land was forgotten, the word drifted to 一生, “a whole life”. Both are correct, but NHK and most newspapers now use 一生懸命.

貧乏暇なし: when you’re poor, just making a living takes all your time, and there’s none to spare.

Looking up, or reading? Looking a word up is 辞書を引く: one of the dictionary senses of 引く is leafing through a book to find a word. Peter’s 辞書を読む, reading it like a book, is the unusual part.

Quiz

Pick the word or form that fits. Each answer comes with the reason.

1. 健康けんこうのために、毎日まいにち野菜やさいを食たべる＿＿＿。

For my health, I make a point of eating vegetables every day.

A habit you keep up: 〜ようにしている. ことになっている is a rule someone else set.

2. 夜よるは、スマホを見み＿＿＿ようにしている。

At night, I make a point of not looking at my phone.

Make a point of not doing something: 〜ないようにする.

3. 明日あしたは遅おくれない＿＿＿。

Please make sure you’re not late tomorrow.

〜ようにしてください: please make a point of it, a polite instruction.

4. 毎日まいにち少すこしずつ＿＿＿。

I make a point of getting a little exercise every day.

An effort kept up every day: ようにしている. てみる is trying something once.

5. 窓まどを開あけて、風かぜが通とおる＿＿＿。

I opened the window so the air could flow through.

Making a situation happen, with its own subject (風が): the notes’ second use of ようにする.

6. 日本にほんに住すんで一年いちねん、漢字かんじがだいぶ読よめる＿＿＿。

After a year in Japan, I can read a fair amount of kanji.

A change that happened: ようになる, lesson 9’s grammar.

7. ＿＿＿、昨日きのうの試合しあい、見みた？

By the way, did you see yesterday’s match?

ところで changes the subject.

8. 昔むかしの電話でんわ番号ばんごう、まだ＿＿＿？

Do you still remember your old phone number?

Still having it in your head: 覚えている. 覚える is learning it.

9. プレゼント、＿＿＿もらえてうれしいです。

I’m so glad you liked the present.

気に入ってもらえてうれしい: “I’m glad you liked it”.

10. この店みせは、私わたしの＿＿＿のカフェです。

This is my favourite café.

お気に入り: a favourite.

11. 「もう、遅おそいよ！」「それは＿＿＿だよ。一時間いちじかんも待まったんだから。」

“You’re so late!” “That’s my line. I’ve been waiting an hour.”

それはこっちのセリフだ: “that’s what I should be saying”.

12. 見みた目めは小ちいさいけど、この車くるまは＿＿＿広ひろい。

It looks small, but this car is surprisingly roomy.

意外と or 意外に: surprisingly.

13. 姉あねは勉べん強きょう＿＿＿で、いつも図書館としょかんにいる。

My sister is so studious, she’s always in the library.

勉強熱心: studious.

14. 分わからない言葉ことばがあったら、辞書じしょを＿＿＿みよう。

If there’s a word you don’t know, try looking it up.

Looking a word up: 辞書を引く. Reading it cover to cover, like Peter, is 辞書を読む.
